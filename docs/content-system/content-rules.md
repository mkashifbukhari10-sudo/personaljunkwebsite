# Content Rules — Junk Services Dubai

**Status:** Permanent editorial and SEO rulebook. Every article written for this site must follow it.
**Authority:** Subordinate to [`keywords.md`](./keywords.md) on all keyword, mapping and cannibalization matters. Where this file and `keywords.md` appear to disagree about *what to target*, `keywords.md` wins. This file governs *how to write it*.
**Site:** https://junkservicesdubai.com · Blog lives at `/blog/{slug}`
**Created:** 2026-09-20

> **Read this before writing anything.** Not after drafting, not while editing. Most of the failures this document prevents are structural — wrong keyword, wrong page, wrong intent, duplicated purpose — and they cannot be fixed by rewriting sentences.

---

## How to use this document

Content production runs in four phases. **Do not start a later phase until the earlier one is complete.**

```
PHASE 1 — MAP        Open keywords.md. Fill the Pre-Write Mapping Block (§2).
                     No mapping block = no article. Stop if the keyword is not mapped.
                        │
                        ▼
PHASE 2 — CLEAR      Check status (§8), cannibalization (§11), facts (§7).
                     BLOCKED means stop — resolve the block, do not write around it.
                        │
                        ▼
PHASE 3 — WRITE      Intent first (§5), human voice (§9), honest claims (§6),
                     contextual links (§12–14), depth to match the query (§22).
                        │
                        ▼
PHASE 4 — QA         Run the full pre-publish checklist (§24).
                     Identify the reverse link (§13). Then publish.
```

## §1 · Core content objective

The business objective is **qualified junk-removal enquiries in Dubai**. Nothing else.

Content exists to support this journey:

```
search query → useful content → relevant service or area page → WhatsApp / phone / contact → qualified lead
```

**These are not success metrics:** article count · word count · traffic volume · keyword rankings in isolation · publishing streaks.

**These are:** enquiries that the crew can actually service, arriving from organic search.

Every article's job is to **strengthen a money page** defined in `keywords.md`. An article that attracts readers but never moves anyone toward a service page has failed at its actual purpose, however well written it is.

A practical consequence: when choosing between two possible topics, prefer the one closer to a real removal decision, even if the other looks like it would attract more traffic.

## §2 · `keywords.md` is the source of truth

**Every article derives its keyword target from `keywords.md`.** That file holds 283 researched, relevant and mapped Dubai keyword opportunities, the Top 10 focus set, page-level keyword ownership, the C-series cannibalization rulings and the article status system. **Do not re-do keyword research. Do not invent targets.**

### The Pre-Write Mapping Block

Before writing a single sentence, complete this. **An article without a completed mapping block does not get written.**

```
PRIMARY KEYWORD:        (exact entry from the keywords.md database, with its cluster ID e.g. C4, E6)
SECONDARY KEYWORDS:     (only those genuinely relevant; from the same or adjacent cluster)
SEARCH INTENT:          (instructions / options / cost / rules / comparison / urgent /
                         move-out / access / commercial / service info)
CONTENT CLUSTER:        (SC1–SC6)
CANONICAL TARGET URL:   (/blog/{slug} — the article's own URL)
PRIMARY MONEY PAGE:     (exactly one; goes in the Payload `relatedServices` field)
SECONDARY SERVICE PAGES:(where genuinely relevant; may be none)
RELEVANT AREA PAGES:    (only if the article genuinely discusses them; usually none)
RELATED EXISTING ARTICLES: (for lateral links; may be none early on)
CANNIBALIZATION RISKS:  (which C-series rulings apply; what this URL uniquely satisfies)
CONTENT STATUS:         (READY / READY — BUSINESS INPUT REQUIRED /
                         BLOCKED — SOURCE VERIFICATION / BLOCKED — SERVICE CAPABILITY /
                         DO NOT TARGET)
```

### Hard rules

1. **Never introduce a new primary keyword without first mapping it in `keywords.md`.** If a genuinely new opportunity appears, it goes through the admission process in *Data-Driven Keyword Expansion* in `keywords.md` — six admission tests, a destination URL, and a cannibalization check — **before** an article is commissioned for it.
2. **Never create a new URL merely because wording differs.** "Furniture removal" and "old furniture removal" are the same job. "Villa clearance" and "villa junk removal" are the same job. This is the single most common way content programmes destroy their own rankings.
3. **Respect all existing ownership decisions** in the *Service Keyword Clusters*, *Existing Page Keyword Ownership* and *Keyword-to-Page Map* tables.
4. **If a proposed article's primary keyword appears in the `Secondary` column of a service cluster, the article is redundant.** Strengthen the service page instead.
5. **If a proposed article conflicts with the homepage, a service page, an area page or an existing article — STOP.** Resolve the cannibalization before writing. Do not write it and hope.

### What to do when the rules block you

Escalate, don't improvise. The three legitimate outcomes when an article cannot proceed are:

- **Fold it into an existing page** as a new section or FAQ.
- **Park it** until the blocking input arrives.
- **Drop it** and record why.

"Reword it until it seems different enough" is not an outcome. It produces the exact near-duplicate the mapping was designed to prevent.

## §3 · Dubai-only scope

Target **Dubai only**.

**Do not target:** Abu Dhabi · Sharjah · Ajman · Ras Al Khaimah · Fujairah · Umm Al Quwain · Al Ain · generic UAE-wide service terms.

These may be **mentioned** where genuinely factual or contextual — for example, a UAE-level regulation that happens to apply in Dubai, or a charity that operates across several emirates. Mentioning is not targeting: it must not appear in the title, H1, meta description or as a heading, and it must not shape the article's angle.

Dubai communities may be discussed whenever relevant. But:

- **Do not force "Dubai" or area names repeatedly into copy.** If a sentence reads worse with the city name in it, leave it out. The page already establishes its geography.
- **Local relevance comes from genuinely useful Dubai-specific information** — how tower service lifts are booked, what gated-community access involves, what a villa handover requires, what the bins will and won't take — **not from repeating place names.**

A useful test: if the article would read identically with "Dubai" swapped for "London", it has no local relevance, only local keywords. Fix the substance, not the keyword count.

## §4 · Strict business-intent boundary

The business provides **junk removal and clearance**. Nothing else.

### Never target buyer or seller intent

| Excluded | Examples |
|---|---|
| Selling | sell my furniture dubai · sell my sofa dubai · where to sell old furniture dubai |
| Buying | used furniture dubai · second-hand furniture dubai · buy used appliances dubai |
| Buyers / traders | used furniture buyers dubai · who buys used furniture in dubai |
| Scrap trade | scrap buyers dubai · scrap metal dealers dubai · sell scrap dubai |
| Valuation | how much is my old furniture worth dubai |
| Marketplaces | dubizzle furniture · facebook marketplace dubai · olx dubai |

The full exclusion list — including moving/relocation services, truck and skip rental, cleaning services, out-of-Dubai terms, employment and franchise queries — is in *Excluded Search Intent* in `keywords.md`. **All of it applies here.**

### Adjacent services

Movers, cleaners, handymen, landscapers and charities may be **mentioned** when genuinely useful to the reader — a move-out article that pretends professional movers don't exist is a worse article. But:

- **They must never become SEO targets.**
- **Never imply the business provides them.** If the business does not do end-of-tenancy cleaning, the article must not be written in a way that reads as though it might.

### The "free junk removal" boundary

`free junk removal dubai` and its variants are **informational support only, never a money target**. The intent is legitimate removal intent, but the searcher expects free collection and this business charges. Handle it with one honest comparison article that explains what is genuinely free in Dubai, what those free routes will and won't do, and when a paid crew is the right answer.

**Never imply this service is free.** Not in a title, not in a meta description, not in a heading, not by omission.

## §5 · Search intent first

**Every article must solve the actual query behind its primary keyword.** Not the topic. The query.

### Identify the intent before writing

Determine which of these the reader wants:

| Intent | What the reader needs first |
|---|---|
| **Instructions** | The steps, in order |
| **Disposal options** | The realistic list, compared honestly |
| **Cost information** | A real number or the variables that decide it |
| **Rules / regulations** | What is allowed, what isn't, what the penalty is |
| **Comparison** | A straight side-by-side, including when *not* to choose us |
| **Urgent help** | What is possible today, and by when |
| **Move-out guidance** | What has to be gone, and the deadline working backwards |
| **Building / access guidance** | Whether it can physically be done, and what it requires |
| **Commercial clearance** | Disruption, timing, scale and who handles what |
| **Service information** | What the service covers and what it costs to start |

### Structure follows intent

- **Answer the core question early** — in the first screen, before any scrolling. An instructions article opens with the first step or a short summary of the route. A cost article opens with the cost position. A rules article opens with the rule.
- **No warm-up.** No paragraph explaining that Dubai is a busy city, that clutter accumulates, or that disposal can be confusing. The reader already knows; that is why they searched.
- **Do not turn every informational article into a sales page.** An article that answers a question honestly and then offers help converts better than one that withholds the answer to force an enquiry — and it is the only version that earns links.
- **Commercial CTAs come after the useful information**, as a natural next step, not as an interruption (see §17).

## §6 · E-E-A-T and the trust standard

Follow Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trust principles — **but never manufacture any of them.**

### Never invent

Jobs completed · customer experiences · case studies · reviews · testimonials · crew anecdotes · years of experience · certifications · licences · disposal partnerships · recycling percentages · Municipality relationships · customer numbers · response times · service availability · prices · statistics · awards · team size · fleet size.

### Banned phrasings (unless the evidence genuinely exists)

- "We've seen hundreds of…"
- "Our customers usually…"
- "From our years of experience…"
- "Our crews report that…"
- "In our experience, most Dubai residents…"
- "We've cleared thousands of…"
- "Clients often tell us…"

These are the default register of AI-written service content, and they are all fabrications unless someone at the business actually said them. **If a claim would need a source and there isn't one, cut the claim — do not soften it into vagueness.** "Many people find…" is the same fabrication with the number removed.

### How to demonstrate expertise honestly

- **Accurate process information** — what actually happens on a pickup, in what order, with what constraints. This is knowable from the service pages and from the business.
- **Useful explanations** — *why* a fridge can't go in a skip, *why* a sofa has to be dismantled, *why* a service lift needs booking.
- **Verified local knowledge** — building access, community rules, handover requirements, what the bins take. Verified, not assumed.
- **Honest limits** — saying what the service will not take, or when a free route is the better choice, builds more trust than any invented statistic.

### Authorship

Every post requires an author (a required relationship in the CMS). **The byline must be a real person at the business.** Do not invent an author persona, a job title, a bio or credentials. If no real author is available, that is a business-input dependency that blocks publishing — see Appendix B.

## §7 · Factual verification

Treat every factual claim according to the **evidence label system** in `keywords.md` (`VERIFIED FACT` · `QUALITATIVE SEO ASSESSMENT` · `COMMERCIAL HYPOTHESIS` · `UNVERIFIED SEARCH-DEMAND ASSUMPTION`).

### Claims requiring verification before publication

These must be checked against a reliable primary source **every time**, even if they seem obvious and even if a competitor has already published them:

Dubai Municipality services and channels · waste regulations · dumping rules and prohibitions · fines and penalties · bulky-waste collection (eligibility, process, timescales, exclusions) · recycling routes and facilities · e-waste rules · appliance and refrigerant disposal requirements · charity collection services (names, numbers, coverage, timescales) · NOC requirements · building and community rules · free-zone and development-zone rules · legal and tenancy requirements · any government service.

### Source hierarchy

1. **Official Dubai / UAE government sources** — Dubai Municipality, the relevant authority's own site or app, official announcements.
2. **Official organisations** — the charity's own website for its own collection service; a manufacturer for its own product.
3. **Authoritative primary sources** — published regulations, official documentation.
4. **Reputable secondary sources** — established news outlets, used only where no primary source exists, and identified as secondary.

**Never cite a low-quality SEO site, a competitor blog or a content farm for a regulatory fact.** Competitors in this niche are frequently wrong; copying them inherits both the error and the liability.

### Rules of use

- **Paraphrase accurately.** Never copy source text at length. Explain it in the article's own words, correctly.
- **Cite and link the source** where it helps the reader verify or act — especially where they need to go and do something (book a collection, check eligibility).
- **If reliable verification is unavailable: do not guess.** Either omit the claim entirely and write the article without it, or keep the article blocked. There is no third option.
- **Facts age.** Municipality services, numbers and rules change. Any article resting on them carries a re-check obligation — see §25.

## §8 · Article status rules

`keywords.md` assigns a status to every mapped article opportunity. **Only `READY` content proceeds without additional input.**

| Status | Meaning | What must happen first |
|---|---|---|
| **`READY`** | Writable now from operational knowledge the business already has. | Nothing. Proceed. |
| **`READY — BUSINESS INPUT REQUIRED`** | Structure is sound; the business must supply real figures or specifics — prices, durations, capacities, acceptance lists, availability. | **Obtain the information before making the relevant claims.** Not before writing the whole article — before writing *those claims*. If the input never arrives, the article does not publish. |
| **`BLOCKED — SOURCE VERIFICATION REQUIRED`** | Depends on external facts: Municipality, legal, regulatory, disposal rules, penalties, charity details. | **Verify against a primary source and cite it.** Then the status changes and the article proceeds. |
| **`BLOCKED — SERVICE CAPABILITY CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`** | Depends on a claim about what the business actually does — licensed handling, specific disposal chains, construction/demolition waste, 24-hour or weekend availability, recurring contracts, data-secure IT disposal. | **Confirm the business genuinely provides it and can evidence it.** If it cannot, remove the claim or drop the article. |
| **`DO NOT TARGET`** | Excluded intent, or would cannibalize a money page. | Never proceeds. |

### The cardinal rule

**Never silently convert a blocked article into `READY`.**

Specifically, these are all violations:

- Writing the article and hedging the blocked claim into vagueness.
- Dropping the blocked fact and publishing a hollowed-out article that no longer answers the query.
- Sourcing the blocked fact from a competitor's blog.
- Deciding the fact is "common knowledge".
- Rewriting the article's angle to avoid the fact while keeping the same keyword — if the keyword's intent *requires* the fact, the article still fails.

A status changes only when the underlying information is genuinely obtained, and the change is recorded in `keywords.md`.

### Current blocking reality

At the time of writing there are **45 mapped article opportunity IDs**, of which **44 are independently commissionable** — #23 is consolidated into #6 and produces no separate article. Across those 44: **19 `READY` · 6 `READY — BUSINESS INPUT REQUIRED` · 15 `BLOCKED — SOURCE VERIFICATION` · 4 `BLOCKED — SERVICE CAPABILITY`.** Three of the four originally planned Week 1 pillars are blocked. **Start with `READY` items and work the blocks in parallel** — do not stall the calendar waiting, and do not force the blocked ones through.

## §9 · Natural, human-quality writing

Write for a real Dubai resident or business owner. Someone with a specific problem, limited patience, and a phone in their hand.

### The content should be

Clear · specific · useful · natural · concise where possible · detailed where necessary · easy to scan · locally relevant · commercially aware without reading like an advertisement.

### Banned openings

Never open an article — or a section — with any of these, or a close variant:

- "Are you looking for…"
- "In today's fast-paced world…"
- "When it comes to…"
- "If you're searching for…"
- "Whether you're…"
- "Dubai is a bustling city…"
- "Getting rid of unwanted items can be challenging…"
- "Let's dive in…"
- "In this comprehensive guide, we'll explore…"
- "Look no further…"

### Other AI tells to avoid

- **Rhetorical questions as section openers.** "But what about the cost?" Just state the cost.
- **Tricolon padding** — "fast, reliable and affordable", "safe, efficient and eco-friendly". Three adjectives where one specific fact would do more.
- **Hedging stacks** — "it's generally often the case that…"
- **Summary paragraphs that restate the article** with no new information.
- **"It's important to note that…"** — if it's important, just say it.
- **Empty transitions** — "Additionally", "Moreover", "Furthermore" opening consecutive paragraphs.
- **Manufactured symmetry** — every section exactly three bullets, every article exactly five headings.

### Structural variety is a rule, not a preference

**Do not use the same template for every article.** Vary:

- **Opening** — a direct answer, a specific scenario, the rule, the cost, the constraint.
- **Structure** — steps for instructions; a comparison table for options; a decision path for "which should I choose"; short problem/answer blocks for troubleshooting.
- **Heading style** — questions for some, statements for others, noun phrases where that reads better.
- **Ending** — some articles end with the next step, some with a limit or caveat, some with the single most useful fact. **Not every article needs a conclusion**, and a conclusion that adds nothing should be deleted.

If two articles in the same cluster could swap their introductions without anyone noticing, both need rewriting.

### Voice

Match the existing site copy, which is plain, direct and confident without being salesy — short sentences, concrete nouns, no hype. The service pages are the reference. Read one before writing.

## §10 · Keyword usage

Primary keywords should appear **naturally** where appropriate:

- SEO title
- H1 (the post title)
- Introduction, where it fits the sentence
- At least one relevant heading or body passage, where it fits
- Meta description / excerpt

**But natural language always takes priority over keyword placement.** If the keyword does not fit a sentence, the sentence wins.

### Prohibited

- **Keyword density targets.** There is no percentage. There never was.
- **Unnatural exact-match repetition.** If "furniture removal Dubai" appears six times, five of them are wrong.
- **Paragraphs written to host a keyword.** If a paragraph exists to place a phrase rather than to inform, delete it.
- **Keyword-stuffed headings.** Headings describe the section; they are not placement slots.
- **Keyword-stuffed image alt text.** Alt text describes the image for someone who cannot see it. That is its only job.
- **Writing "junk removal near me" into copy.** It is resolved by proximity and Google Business Profile signals, not page text.

### Semantic and secondary terms

Use related vocabulary where it genuinely improves clarity — *dismantle*, *service lift*, *handover*, *bulky waste*, *green waste*, *strip-out*, *e-waste*. This is how a page demonstrates it actually understands the topic.

**Secondary keywords are incorporated only where relevant.** A secondary keyword that doesn't fit the article is simply not used. It stays mapped in `keywords.md` for whichever page genuinely owns it.

## §11 · Cannibalization prevention

**Before writing any article, check the complete current site and content inventory.** Not a memory of it — the actual current state.

### Compare the proposed topic against

1. **The homepage** — owns `junk removal dubai` (head term) and brand terms.
2. **All 12 service pages** — each owns its commercial cluster.
3. **All 30 area pages** — each owns `junk removal {area}` and its close variants.
4. **Every published blog article.**
5. **Every planned article** in the `keywords.md` Article Opportunity Map.

### The one question that decides it

> **"What unique search intent does this URL satisfy that no other page on this site does?"**

**If there is no clear, specific answer — do not create the article.**

Answers that do not count:
- "It targets a different keyword." (Different wording is not different intent.)
- "It goes into more detail." (Then improve the existing page.)
- "It's for a different area." (See §15.)
- "It approaches it from a different angle." (If the reader's question is the same, the page is the same.)

### Standing rules

- **Supporting blog content reinforces money pages. It never competes with them.**
- **All C-series rulings in `keywords.md` are binding** — C-1 through C-14. Read the relevant one before writing in that territory.
- **C-1 and C-2 are marked PRE-PUBLISH SEO REVIEW.** They involve head-term and same-day ownership across the homepage, `/services`, `/services/junk-removal` and `/services/same-day-junk-removal`. **Both must be reviewed and signed off before large-scale supporting-content publication begins**, because articles amplify whichever ownership signals exist when they are published.

### When you find an overlap

Resolve it before writing, using the same hierarchy `keywords.md` uses: **secondary keyword on the existing owner** is the default resolution. A new page requires genuinely distinct intent *and* enough unique substance to stand alone. Wording difference is never sufficient.

## §12 · Internal linking — core requirement

Internal linking is **mandatory**. The *number* of links is **not**.

> **Relevance determines quantity.** A short single-item article may warrant two or three links. A long pillar guide may warrant a dozen. **Never insert a link to satisfy a count.** An irrelevant link wastes the reader's attention, dilutes the signal the link is meant to carry, and at scale looks manipulative. If a link would not help a reader who has never heard of SEO, it does not go in.

### Before finalising an article, inspect the current inventory and identify

| | What to identify | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary money page** | The one service page this article supports | **Exactly one. Mandatory.** Declared in the Payload `relatedServices` field and linked contextually in the body. |
| **Secondary service pages** | Other services the article genuinely touches | Where useful. May be none. |
| **Area pages** | Communities the article genuinely discusses | **Only when genuinely relevant.** Most articles link to none. |
| **Related existing articles** | Pieces that extend the reader's journey | Where useful. May be none early on. |

### Rules

1. **Every article has exactly one clearly defined primary money-page relationship.** Link to it contextually, at the point where the reader's problem meets the service — usually early-to-middle, in a sentence that would exist anyway. **Never as a footer link block.**
2. **Secondary service links where useful.** No minimum, no maximum.
3. **Area links only when the article genuinely discusses that community, property type, access condition or logistics.** A mention of the area name is not enough — the article must say something specific about it.
4. **Never append lists of Dubai areas for SEO.** This is the single most recognisable spam pattern in this niche, and it is explicitly forbidden.
5. **Related-article links where they extend the journey** — the reader's obvious next question, not a "related posts" dump.
6. **Use the CMS relationship fields**, not just body links. `relatedServices` and `relatedAreas` on the Posts collection exist for exactly this.

## §13 · Two-way internal linking

**Do not treat internal linking as one-directional.** A new article that links out to five pages and receives nothing back is an orphan with good manners.

### After creating a new article, identify what should link back to it

Candidate reverse-link sources, in rough order of value:

1. **The primary service page** — at the point in its existing copy where the article's topic naturally comes up. `keywords.md` contains a *Reverse-link opportunities from existing content* table mapping each service page's existing sections to the articles that should link down from them. **Use it.**
2. **A related service page** — where the topic genuinely spans both.
3. **A relevant area page** — only where the article is genuinely about that community.
4. **An older related article** — the earlier piece in the same cluster, updated to point forward.
5. **Cluster or pillar content** — the pillar guide for that cluster.

### Rules

- **Every published article must have at least one meaningful inbound internal link.** Not the blog index — that link exists automatically and does not count. A contextual link from a relevant page.
- **No orphan articles.** If no existing page would naturally link to it, that is strong evidence the article does not belong in the architecture. Re-examine whether it should exist.
- **Recommend or apply reverse links where they genuinely improve navigation and topical relationships** — never by bolting a link list onto a service page.
- **Reverse-linking is part of the article's definition of done**, not a later cleanup pass. An article is not finished until its inbound link is identified.
- **Keep crawl depth sensible** — every article reachable from the homepage within three clicks, via `/blog` or its money page.

## §14 · Anchor text rules

Anchor text must be **descriptive, natural, contextually useful and varied**.

### Do

- Write the sentence first, then link the phrase that already describes the destination.
- Vary the form: descriptive ("have a crew carry it out"), partial-match ("sofa removal in the Marina"), branded, and plain natural phrases.
- Let the surrounding sentence carry the context.

### Don't

- **Repeatedly link with exact-match commercial anchors** — "junk removal dubai", "furniture removal dubai", "villa clearance dubai". Exact-match anchors may occur naturally; they must never become a pattern.
- **Use the same anchor sitewide** for the same destination.
- **Use bare "click here" or "read more"** — they describe nothing.
- **Link an entire sentence.**
- **Stack multiple links in one sentence**, which makes all of them easy to ignore.

### A useful check

Read the article's anchors as a list, with no surrounding text. If they read like a keyword list rather than a set of natural phrases, rewrite them.

## §15 · Area-page protection

The 30 existing `/areas/{slug}` pages **own local service intent**. They are genuinely area-specific — service-lift booking in Dubai Marina, gate access on Palm Jumeirah, warehouse clear-outs in Al Quoz — not templated doorway pages.

### Do NOT create articles like

- "Junk Removal in JVC"
- "Junk Removal in Al Barsha"
- "Junk Removal in Mirdif"
- "Best Junk Removal Services in Dubai Marina"

**The area page already serves that intent.** Publishing these would create doorway pages competing with the site's own area layer and risk a manual action. This is ruling **C-12** in `keywords.md`, and it is one of the strictest rules in the system.

**Default position: no per-area articles.** Strengthen the area page instead.

### Area-specific articles are allowed only when

The article answers a **genuinely different question** and carries informational value beyond changing the area name. Legitimate examples:

- **Tower and service-lift logistics** — how booking actually works, what buildings require.
- **Access requirements** — gated communities, permits, timing windows.
- **Verified disposal-rule differences** — e.g. communities reportedly outside the free bulky-waste service. **Only if verified** (§7).
- **Property-specific clearance guidance** — what clearing a townhouse involves versus a tower apartment.

### The test

> Could this article be published with a different area name substituted throughout, and still be accurate?

**If yes, it is a doorway page. Do not publish it.**

## §16 · Service-page protection

Commercial service pages **own their transactional keywords**. Blog articles support them; they never compete.

### The governing pattern

| | |
|---|---|
| **Service page** | *Furniture Removal Dubai* — owns `furniture removal dubai`, the commercial intent, the price and booking path |
| **Supporting article** | *How to Dispose of Old Furniture in Dubai* — owns the research-stage question, compares the real options honestly, and guides readers to the service page when a paid crew is the right answer |

The article **educates first**, then guides naturally toward the service. It does not open with a pitch, and it does not try to rank for `furniture removal dubai`.

### Rules

1. **Never write a blog post designed to rank for a service page's exact commercial intent.**
2. **An article's title and H1 must not read as a service offer** — "Furniture Removal Services in Dubai" is a service page title, not an article title.
3. **If an article starts drifting into service-page territory** — listing what's included, the price, the booking steps as its main content — **stop.** That material belongs on the service page. Link to it.
4. **If the research shows the service page is weak**, the correct response is to improve the service page, not to write an article that outranks it. A blog post ranking for a commercial term converts worse than a service page ranking for it.

## §17 · CTA rules

CTAs must match the reader's stage and the article's context.

### Available actions

WhatsApp · phone call · request a quote · contact form · the relevant service page.

The site's primary conversion path is **WhatsApp with a photo**, which is also the lowest-friction ask. The `/contact` form both records the enquiry and hands off to WhatsApp.

### Placement by intent

| Article intent | CTA approach |
|---|---|
| **Informational** (rules, options, where things go) | Educate fully first. One natural transition toward help, near or at the end, once the reader has what they came for. |
| **Problem-solving with urgency** (sofa won't fit, inspection tomorrow) | A CTA may appear earlier — the reader has an immediate need and offering help *is* the useful answer. |
| **Cost / comparison** | After the honest answer, including when the answer is "a free route is better for you". |
| **Commercial / B2B** | Later and more consultative. B2B readers are researching, not booking. |

### Rules

- **Avoid excessive CTA repetition.** One well-placed CTA outperforms four.
- **Do not interrupt informational sections every few paragraphs with sales messages.** This is the fastest way to lose both the reader and the link.
- **Never make false urgency claims** — no invented scarcity, no "limited slots today" unless it is literally true, no countdown pressure.
- **Never promise a response time, availability or price** that the business has not confirmed.
- **The CTA should read as help, not as a pitch.** "Send a photo and we'll price it" is an offer of usefulness. "Contact us today for the best junk removal in Dubai!" is noise.

---

## §18 · Pricing content

**Never invent prices or price ranges.** Not a range, not a "starting from", not a "typically around", not a competitor's number repurposed.

### Rules

1. **If an article targets cost or pricing and real business pricing is unavailable, it stays `READY — BUSINESS INPUT REQUIRED`.** It is not written with hedged language instead.
2. **If pricing depends on variables, explain only the verified variables** — volume, access, floor level, dismantling, crew size, disposal type. Explaining *what drives* a price is genuinely useful and requires no invented numbers. This is often the better article anyway.
3. **Do not publish vague fabricated ranges to satisfy a cost keyword.** A cost article with made-up numbers is worse than no cost article: it misleads the reader, produces enquiries with wrong expectations, and damages trust at the exact moment it was meant to build it.
4. **Any price that does appear must be one the business will honour.** If it changes, the article is updated (§25).
5. **Never state or imply that a paid service is free.**

---

## §19 · FAQ rules

**FAQs are optional.** They are not a required section, a ranking device or a word-count filler.

### Use an FAQ only when

It answers a **genuine follow-up question** that the main body does not already answer clearly — the thing a reader would still wonder about after finishing.

### Do not

- **Create FAQs solely for SEO or schema.**
- **Repeat the article body in question-and-answer form.** If it's answered above, the FAQ is duplication.
- **Force a fixed number.** Three is not a target. Zero is a valid answer.
- **Invent questions nobody asks** — "What is junk removal?" on an article about fridge disposal.
- **Use FAQs to smuggle in keywords.**

### Quality bar

Questions should reflect **realistic user concerns**, phrased the way a person would actually ask. The existing service and area pages contain good examples — "My sofa will not fit through the door. Can you still take it?", "Do you book the service lift with my building?" Those are real questions with real answers.

### Schema — an important technical constraint

**FAQ schema is currently emitted for the homepage, service pages and area pages only.** Blog posts have no `faqs` field and the post template emits `BlogPosting` schema only. **An article cannot produce FAQ schema today without a code change.**

Therefore: write FAQs when they genuinely help the reader, and treat schema as out of scope for articles. Do not request a code change for this unless there is a clear reason beyond "for schema" — and note that Google has substantially reduced FAQ rich-result display in any case.

## §20 · External links and sources

Use external sources when they **materially improve factual trust**.

### Source preference

1. **Official Dubai / UAE government sources** — the authority's own site, app or published material.
2. **Official organisations** — a charity's own site for its own collection service.
3. **Authoritative primary sources** — published regulations, official documentation, manufacturer guidance.
4. **Reputable secondary sources** — established news outlets, only where no primary source exists, and identified as such.

### Rules

- **Do not link competitors** to make an article look researched. It sends readers to a rival at the exact moment they are deciding.
- **Do not cite low-quality SEO sites or content farms for regulatory facts.** Wrong information is common in this niche.
- **External sources support factual claims; they do not replace original explanation.** An article that is mostly links to other people's explanations has no reason to exist.
- **Link where the reader needs to act** — if they need to book a Municipality collection, link them to the official channel. Sending someone to the right place is useful even when it isn't a sale.
- **Check links resolve** before publishing, and re-check when updating (§25).

---

## §21 · Originality

**Never copy competitor articles.**

### Competitor research is legitimate for

- Understanding **search intent** — what the SERP suggests readers want.
- Identifying **missing questions** nobody has answered well.
- Gauging **topic coverage** expectations.
- Understanding **SERP format** expectations (list, guide, comparison).

### It is not legitimate for

- **Copying text**, in whole or in part.
- **Paraphrasing section-by-section** — rewriting someone else's article in different words is still their article.
- **Imitating heading structure**, which is the skeleton of their argument.
- **Reproducing their wording or framing.**
- **Inheriting their facts.** Competitors in this niche are frequently wrong about Municipality rules. Every fact must be independently verified (§7) regardless of how many competitors repeat it.

### The standard

The final content must be **independently written** from an understanding of the topic and the reader — not assembled from what already ranks. In a market where the competitor field is largely template-spun near-duplicates, genuine originality is the most realistic competitive advantage this site has.

---

## §22 · Article depth

**There is no universal word-count requirement.** Not 1,000. Not 1,500. Not 2,000.

### The rule

**Length follows search intent and topic complexity.** An article should be as long as necessary to answer the query properly — **and no longer.**

- **Do not inflate** with filler, restated points, padded introductions, unnecessary background or summary sections that add nothing.
- **Do not artificially shorten** a complex topic. A genuinely complicated subject — villa clearance logistics, e-waste rules — needs the room to be correct.
- **Useful coverage beats word count**, always.

### Practical guidance

| Article type | Typical shape |
|---|---|
| Single-item disposal ("how to get rid of a mattress") | Short and direct. The answer, the options, the constraints. |
| Problem/troubleshooting ("sofa won't fit") | Short to medium. Diagnosis, options, what happens next. |
| Rules / regulatory | Medium. Accuracy and sourcing matter far more than length. |
| Comparison | Medium. Structured, honest, with a clear decision path. |
| Pillar guide | Longer, because it genuinely covers more — never because it is a pillar. |

**If a section could be deleted without the reader losing anything, delete it.** This applies to the conclusion more often than anywhere else.

---

## §23 · Daily publishing standard

**Publishing capacity: UP TO 3 high-quality articles per day.**

**This is a ceiling, not a quota.** The business would like to reach three. It must never become a requirement that produces thin, repetitive or overlapping content.

### Every article must pass all eight gates

1. **Distinct search intent** — not a rewording of an intent already covered.
2. **Mapped primary keyword** — drawn from `keywords.md`.
3. **Unique purpose** — answers something no existing page answers.
4. **Defined money-page relationship** — exactly one, declared in `relatedServices`.
5. **Enough useful information to justify its own URL** — if the answer is three sentences, it belongs as a section or FAQ on an existing page.
6. **No material cannibalization** with any service page, area page or existing article.
7. **Factual requirements satisfied** — status is `READY`, or the blocking input has genuinely been obtained.
8. **Appropriate internal-link opportunities** — a primary money page, and at least one identified inbound link so it will not be orphaned.

> These extend the six gates in `keywords.md` by adding factual clearance (7) and link integrity (8). They are a superset, not a contradiction.

### How the rule operates

- **If three articles pass, publish three.**
- **If two pass, publish two.**
- **If one passes, publish one.**
- **If none pass, publish none** — and spend the day on verification, business input, internal linking or improving existing pages.

**Never create a third weak article to meet a target.** An article that fails a gate is not published, not reworded into eligibility, and not swapped for a filler topic. Falling short of three because topics did not clear the gates is **a correct outcome, not a failure** — and it should be reported as such rather than hidden by lowering the bar.

## §24 · Pre-publish content QA

Run this in full on every article. **Any failure blocks publication.**

### SEARCH

- [ ] Primary keyword exists in `keywords.md` and is mapped to this article
- [ ] Search intent correctly identified and genuinely satisfied
- [ ] Correct page type (article, not a service or area page in disguise)
- [ ] No cannibalization with homepage, service pages, area pages, published or planned articles
- [ ] Relevant C-series rulings checked
- [ ] Title and H1 read naturally and are not service-page-style offers
- [ ] Secondary terms used only where relevant, and naturally
- [ ] Meta description / excerpt is accurate, useful and within the CMS limit

### QUALITY

- [ ] The useful answer begins early — within the first screen
- [ ] No filler introduction, no banned opening
- [ ] No repetitive AI phrasing; structure differs from sibling articles
- [ ] **No fabricated experience** — no invented jobs, customers, anecdotes or years
- [ ] **No unsupported business claims** — capability, availability, certification, partnership
- [ ] No keyword stuffing; no paragraph exists to host a keyword
- [ ] No unnecessary repetition; nothing that could be deleted without loss
- [ ] Length matches intent — not padded, not truncated
- [ ] Author is a real person

### FACTS

- [ ] Every regulatory, Municipality, legal, penalty or charity claim verified against a primary source
- [ ] Official sources used and linked where the reader needs to act
- [ ] No fact inherited from a competitor without independent verification
- [ ] Service capabilities confirmed with the business
- [ ] Pricing confirmed where required — **no invented ranges**
- [ ] No copied or closely paraphrased source text

### INTERNAL LINKS

- [ ] Exactly one primary money page identified and set in `relatedServices`
- [ ] Primary money page linked contextually in the body, not in a footer block
- [ ] Relevant secondary service links included where useful
- [ ] **Area links included only where genuinely justified** — no appended area lists
- [ ] `relatedAreas` set only where genuinely relevant
- [ ] Related-article links included where they extend the journey
- [ ] Anchor text natural, descriptive and varied — no mechanical exact-match pattern
- [ ] **At least one inbound/reverse-link opportunity identified**, and applied or logged
- [ ] The article will not be orphaned
- [ ] Crawl depth sensible

### CONVERSION

- [ ] CTA appropriate to intent and stage
- [ ] Not excessive; informational sections not interrupted by selling
- [ ] **No false urgency**, no unconfirmed response times, availability or prices
- [ ] Nothing implies the service is free
- [ ] The reader gets their answer whether or not they convert

## §25 · Post-publish rule

**Publication is not the end of an article's life.**

### Inputs for future optimisation

Search Console queries · impressions · CTR · which URL actually ranks · cannibalization signals (URLs swapping on a query) · new internal-link opportunities as the corpus grows · conversions and leads where measurable · updated official information.

### The default response to a new opportunity is to improve, not to add

> **Do not create a new article merely because Search Console reveals a close keyword variant.**
>
> **First determine whether the existing page should simply be improved.** In almost every case it should. Updating one strong page that already has history, links and authority beats splitting that authority across two weaker ones.

A new article is justified only when the query represents **genuinely distinct intent** that no existing page can satisfy — and even then it must pass the full admission process in `keywords.md` and all eight gates in §23.

### Maintenance obligations

- **Fact-dependent articles carry a re-check obligation.** Municipality services, charity details, rules and penalties change. Any article resting on them must be re-verified periodically and whenever the source changes.
- **Prices must be updated** whenever the business changes them.
- **Broken external links** must be fixed or removed.
- **Reverse links should be revisited** as the corpus grows — an article published in Week 2 may deserve a link from an article published in Week 7.
- **Changing a published slug is safe** — the CMS writes a 301 automatically — but it still costs some signal. Get the slug right the first time.

### Consolidation

If two articles are found to be competing, **consolidate rather than tolerate it**: merge the weaker into the stronger, redirect it, and update `keywords.md` to record the change. Two half-ranking pages are worth less than one that ranks.

---

## §26 · Final principle

> **One excellent page satisfying an intent beats multiple weak pages targeting slight keyword variations.**

Every rule above is a consequence of that sentence.

The goal is a trustworthy Dubai junk-removal knowledge and service ecosystem where:

- **Service pages capture commercial intent** — someone ready to book.
- **Area pages capture genuine local intent** — someone checking whether their building is covered.
- **Articles answer supporting questions** — someone working out what their options are.
- **Internal links connect them naturally** — so the reader moves from question to answer to action without friction.

When a decision is genuinely unclear, resolve it with this question:

> **Does this help a real person in Dubai who needs something taken away?**

If yes, it probably belongs. If the honest answer is "it helps us rank", it does not.

## Appendix A · CMS field contract

Articles are Payload `Posts` documents rendered at `/blog/{slug}`. **These are the real fields and their real constraints** — verified against `src/payload/collections/Posts.ts`. Writing to them correctly is part of the job.

| Field | Type | Required | Rules that apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`title`** | text | **Yes** | This is the article's **H1**. Write it for a reader, not as a keyword slot (§10, §16). Must not read as a service-page offer. |
| **`slug`** | text (sidebar) | **Yes** | Auto-generated from the title if left empty; can be set explicitly. The planned slugs in the `keywords.md` Article Opportunity Map are proposals — **set the slug deliberately** rather than accepting whatever the title generates. Changing a published slug automatically leaves a 301, but get it right first time. |
| **`excerpt`** | textarea | **Yes** | **Max 200 characters; aim for 160.** Shown on `/blog` **and used as the meta description** unless the SEO tab overrides it. So it must work as both a listing teaser and a search snippet — accurate, useful, not clickbait. |
| **`coverImage`** | upload | **Yes, to publish** | Publication is blocked without one; it is also the default social card. Alt text describes the image for someone who cannot see it — **it is not a keyword slot** (§10). |
| **`content`** | richText (Lexical) | **Yes** | **Headings start at H2** — H1 is the title. H2/H3/H4 available. Bold, italic, ordered/unordered lists, blockquote, inline images, horizontal rule. |
| **Links (in `content`)** | Lexical link feature | — | Can link internally to **posts, services and areas** as relationships, or to external URLs. A **`nofollow`** checkbox is available for paid, untrusted or user-submitted destinations — official government and organisation sources do **not** need it. |
| **`author`** | relationship → Authors | **Yes** | **Must be a real person** (§6). Authors carry name, role, bio, photo and `sameAs` profile links — all of which support E-E-A-T, and none of which may be invented. |
| **`publishedAt`** | date | Auto | Set on first publish. Change only to correct the record. |
| **`relatedServices`** | relationship → Services | Effectively yes | **The primary money-page relationship (§12).** Exactly one primary; additional genuinely relevant services may be added. |
| **`relatedAreas`** | relationship → Areas | No | **Only where the article genuinely discusses those areas** (§12, §15). Most articles leave this empty. |
| **SEO group** | group | No | `title`, `description`, `canonical`, `noIndex`, `ogTitle`, `ogDescription`, `ogImage`. Overrides the defaults. Leave empty unless there is a specific reason. |
| **`readingTime`** | number (read-only) | Auto | Counted from the body on save. **Not a target to write toward** (§22). |

**Other platform facts worth knowing:**

- **Drafts are enabled**, with a preview URL. Draft and review before publishing — there is no reason to publish straight to live.
- **Schema:** posts emit `BlogPosting` only. `FAQPage` schema is emitted on the homepage, service pages and area pages — **not on posts** (§19).
- **Revalidation** is automatic on change, for both `/blog` and the post's own URL.
- **Slug changes** are recorded and a 301 is written automatically.

## Appendix B · Known constraints and conflicts

Recorded here because they affect what can actually be produced, and because pretending they don't exist is how blocked work gets forced through.

### B-1 · A real author is required before ANY article can publish

`author` is a required relationship, and §6 forbids inventing a person. **This blocks the entire content programme, not just the source-blocked articles.** At least one real named author — with a genuine role, and ideally a short bio — must be created in the CMS before the first publish.

**Action:** business input required. This is the smallest unblocking task with the largest effect.

### B-2 · A cover image is required before any article can publish

Enforced by field validation. Image sourcing and licensing is therefore a real dependency on every article, not an afterthought. Image strategy is out of scope for this file — it is governed by [`image.md`](./image.md), which now exists and covers sourcing, licensing, provenance, honesty, alt text and QA.

**The dependency has not gone away; it has moved.** `image.md` records two open items that still gate publishing: **I-1**, the unresolved provenance of the existing photo library, and **I-2**, cover-image supply, which depends on it. Until those are settled, **treat cover images as a per-article business input.**

### B-3 · 19 of the 44 commissionable articles are currently blocked

15 on source verification, 4 on service-capability confirmation; a further 6 need business input. (**45 mapped IDs, 44 commissionable** — #23 is consolidated into #6 and is not an independent article.) **Three of the four pillars originally sequenced for Week 1 are blocked.** The Week 1 plan in `keywords.md` already carries this note. **Start with `READY` items; work the verification queue in parallel.**

### B-4 · FAQ schema is not available for articles

`faqPageSchema` exists and is used on the homepage, service pages and area pages, but the Posts collection has no `faqs` field and the post template emits `BlogPosting` only. **An article cannot produce FAQ schema without a code change.** §19 resolves this by treating FAQs as a reader-value decision and schema as out of scope — no code change is requested.

### B-5 · §23 defines eight gates; `keywords.md` defines six

Deliberate. This file adds **factual clearance** (gate 7) and **link integrity** (gate 8). It is a superset — nothing in `keywords.md` is contradicted. If the two files are ever reconciled, eight is the operative number.

### B-6 · C-1 and C-2 must be signed off before large-scale publication

Both are marked **PRE-PUBLISH SEO REVIEW** in `keywords.md`. They concern head-term and same-day ownership across `/`, `/services`, `/services/junk-removal` and `/services/same-day-junk-removal`. Articles amplify whatever ownership signals exist when they publish, so **publishing at volume before sign-off entrenches the ambiguity and makes it expensive to unpick.**

C-2 specifically needs a business decision on the homepage H1 (currently *"Same-day junk removal across Dubai"*): SEO-optimal versus conversion-optimal wording. **Either answer is legitimate** — it just has to be a decision rather than a drift.

### B-7 · Conversion measurement is incomplete

§1 defines success as qualified enquiries, but at the time of writing Plausible is not configured (`NEXT_PUBLIC_PLAUSIBLE_DOMAIN` unset) and direct `wa.me` / `tel:` clicks carry no events. The `/contact` form path is recoverable from the Leads collection; the direct-click path is not.

**Consequence:** for now, article-level conversion attribution will be partial at best. §25's optimisation loop depends on data that does not yet exist. This does not block writing, but it does mean **early optimisation decisions will rest on Search Console signals rather than conversion signals** — and that limitation should be stated whenever results are reported.

### B-8 · No conflict found between these rules and the site or keyword strategy

Checked explicitly: Dubai-only scope, the exclusion set, page-level keyword ownership, the C-series rulings, the status system, the contextual (non-quota) link rules, the up-to-3 ceiling and the money-page-first principle are all consistent between this file, `keywords.md` and the live site architecture. The items above are **dependencies and platform constraints, not contradictions.**
