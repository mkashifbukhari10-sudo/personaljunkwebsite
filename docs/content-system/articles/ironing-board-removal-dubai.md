The item is one ironing board behind a door. The legs, the top or the cover stop the door from opening, or the board fills the only gap beside the door. Empty the cover of anything that is not the board, try the fold the board already has, and say whether it is standing free. That is the job. The rest of the bedroom is not. A household bin is not.

This note is the board. A whole bedroom, with the bed, the wardrobe and the tables, is the [bedroom furniture guide](/blog/bedroom-furniture-removal-dubai). What a household container and a chute are for is the [bins guide](/blog/what-dubai-bins-wont-take). ## Make sure it is an ironing board

An ironing board, for this note, is a long narrow top on legs, made to be stood up and folded flat. It may have a metal mesh top, a solid top, a pointed end, a hanging rail, or a small shelf. Write which one you have. “Full-size, metal legs, cover on, will not latch flat.” “Table-top board, no legs, leaning behind the door.”

A table is not this board. A desk is not this board. A shelf that is part of a cupboard is not this board. If the object has drawers, or it is fixed as furniture you sit at, name it as that furniture and keep it off this note.

A clothes airer or a drying rack is not an ironing board. It has no ironing top. If that is the object behind the door, the [drying-rack note](/blog/drying-rack-removal-dubai) is that rack. Do not follow these steps as if it were a board.

An iron is not the board. The iron is the heated tool that sits on the board or in a cupboard. The acceptance list already groups a clothing iron with small appliances when it leaves in a wider load. This page does not empty an iron, and it does not open a steam tank. If the iron is leaving, it is its own line. Write “iron, separate”. If the iron is staying, put it where you keep it, out of the photos of the board.

A wall-mounted board that folds down from a cupboard door, and is screwed into the wall or the cupboard, is a fitting until it is released. If you can see screws into the wall, write “fixed to the wall, staying” and do not unscrew the wall. This note starts when the board already stands free, or when it is a loose board that only leans. A built-in cupboard is not a board you have created by taking the door off.

A sleeve board, the small one used on a table, is not this problem unless it is the object jammed behind the door. If it is, write “sleeve board” and treat it as a short board with the same rules: nothing hidden in the cover, and no sawing. Do not call a sleeve board a full-size board to make the booking sound larger.

If you are not sure, photograph the top and the legs and write “not sure it is an ironing board”. That is a better line than a wrong name.

## The door is the reason

The board was put behind the door because the room had no other gap. The door then stops against the board, or the board slides into the opening every time the door moves. That is the preparation problem. A board that already stands in the middle of the room, folded flat, with the door clear, is a much smaller job. Say which situation you have. Do not describe a clear board as a blocked door.

Open the door as far as it will go without forcing the board. If the door hits the board, stop. Do not keep pushing. A hollow door bends, and a leg can scratch the floor or snap.

Look at what else is in that gap. A robe, a pair of shoes, a laundry basket, a hanging rail of clothes. Move what is yours to move, and put it where it is not the board. If a basket of clothes is also leaving, it is its own line. Do not call the gap “the ironing board”.

Then see how the board is standing. Leaning, with the legs folded. Standing, with the legs locked open. Lying on its side because it fell. Caught under the bottom of the door. Write that in one line. “Leaning, legs folded, door hits the top end.” “Standing, legs locked, door opens a hand’s width.”

Measure the clear opening you actually have, with the door as open as it will go before it meets the board. Then, after you have moved the board out of that opening, measure the door again. The second figure is the door. The first figure is the door with the board in the way. Both are useful. If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent a door size. This note does not keep a standard.

If the only way to open the door fully is to lift the board first, lift it only when you can see that the legs are not going to spring open into your hands or the glass of the door. A board that springs is a board with a lock that has failed. Write “legs spring open” and do not stand in the arc. Slide it out along the floor if it slides, with the legs still in the position they are already in.

A door closer, a hook or a hanging rail can be the thing that stops the door, not the board. If you remove the board and the door still stops, write what it stops on. Do not take a hook out of the wall to finish the sentence. The hook stays with the room.

## Cover, pad and what people hide in it

The cover is the cloth on the top. People tuck hangers, a spray bottle, pins, a small bag and sometimes a spare key under the edge. Run a hand under the cover before the board is called empty. Take those things out. A key stays with you. A spray bottle is not the board. If it is leaving, it is a small separate line, and it does not ride inside the cover.

If the cover lifts off by the cord or the elastic you already use when you change it, you may take it off. Write “cover off”. Fold the cover and decide. If it is leaving, it can sit with the board, not stuffed into a sack of kitchen rubbish as if it were the board. If you are keeping it for another board, it stays out of the photos. Write “cover kept”.

If the cover is stapled, glued or torn into the pad, leave it on. Write “cover left on”. Do not cut the cover off to make the photo cleaner. Do not soak it. A wet cover is a heavier board and a smell you have not named.

The pad under the cover stays on the board unless it is already loose in your hand. A loose pad can come off. Write “pad loose, removed” or “pad left on”. Do not peel a pad that is still stuck.

Look at the shelf or the rail under the top, if there is one. An iron left on that rail is the iron, not the board. A hanger is a hanger. Empty the rail. Write “rail emptied” or “no rail”.

Do not use the board as a tray for other rubbish. Do not strap a bag of clothes to the top. Hidden weight changes the lift, and the bag is a different line.

## The fold the board already has

Most boards lock open with a lever under the top, and fold flat when that lever is released. Use the lever the way you already do when you put the board away. If it folds, fold it, and keep your hands clear of the legs. Write “folded flat”.

If the lever moves and the legs do not, write “lever moves, legs stay open”. Do not jump on the top. Do not stamp the legs shut. A board that will not latch is still one board, and it is taller and wider than a folded one. That is the fact to measure.

If there is no lever, and the board is the kind that only leans, write “no fold”. A table-top board has no legs to collapse. Measure its length and its width as it is.

A leg that is bent stays bent. Write “one leg bent”. Do not hammer it straight. A missing rubber foot is “foot missing”. Do not unscrew the other feet.

The pointed end and the wide end are different widths. If the board is leaving unfolded, the width that matters is the widest part, including a hanging rail that sticks out. If it is folded, the length of the folded board is the long piece. Measure that length, the thickness of the folded legs, and the width. If a rail or a shelf sticks past the legs, include it.

Then compare those figures with the door you named, in one sentence. “Folded flat, longer than the bathroom door is wide” or “legs stay open, blocks the bedroom door” or “not measured”. Do not guess that a folded board always fits a door. Some do not, once the legs fail to sit flat.

Do not saw the top. Do not cut a leg off to make a shorter board. A cut tube is a sharp end, not a folded board. Do not separate the top from the legs with a tool. If a bolt is already loose and the top is already off in your hands, write “top already separated” and keep the pieces together. Do not start that job because the board looks old.

## Where it has been living

Behind a bedroom door is the usual place. The bedroom guide keeps the bed, the wardrobe, the chest and the rest of the room. This board is one line on that list when the room is being cleared. Come back to this page for the door, the cover and the fold. Do not paste the board into “bedroom” and hope the door is obvious. A chest of drawers is not this page. It is not given a URL here. A camp bed behind a wardrobe is the [camp-bed note](/blog/camp-bed-removal-dubai), with a different fold.

Behind a kitchen door, the board may be next to a broom and a bin. The broom stays or leaves as its own line. The kitchen bin, if it is the small one you still use, stays. Do not tip a kitchen bin onto the board. Do not describe the board as rubbish because it was beside the bin.

Behind a bathroom door, the board may be damp. Write “cover damp” if it is, and do not leave it in a closed bathroom overnight if you can stand it somewhere it can stay as it is. This page does not tell you how to dry a flat, and it does not treat a damp cover as a reason to throw the board down a chute.

In a cupboard, the board may be the thing that stops the cupboard door. Slide it out if it slides. If it is wedged, write “wedged in the cupboard” and photograph it there. Do not take the cupboard door off its hinges. The cupboard stays unless it is a freestanding piece you have already decided is leaving, and that piece is not this note.

On a balcony, a board is unusual and often rusty. If it is simply standing there, the same fold and cover steps apply. A mixed balcony is the balcony guide. One board is still one line. Do not add it to a count of racks and call the count a board.

A board you are moving to another room to keep using is not a collection. This note is for the board that is leaving the property. If someone else wants it, arrange that handover yourself and keep it out of the leaving photos until they have it. A message that says “I might keep it” is not a decision.

## The bins are a different question

A household bin and a chute are for the waste those containers are already used for. The bins guide is where that question is answered, including for furniture that does not belong in a container. This note does not add a rule, and it does not say that an ironing board is or is not accepted by any particular service outside this booking.

Do not put the board in a sack. A board in a sack looks like a bag of clothes, and the legs come through. Do not leave it in the bin room to see who takes it. Do not slide it into a chute. If you were about to do that because the board is thin, stop and use the steps above so the fold and the door are written down instead.

If the board is in a room of bags from a clear-out, lift it out of that pile. A bag of clothes is not the board. A cover you removed is not a bag of clothes unless you have decided the cover is leaving and you have said so.

The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a board is worth.

## One line in the booking

You can [take it with the furniture](/services/furniture-removal) once the cover has been checked, the fold has been tried, and the door it was blocking is named. That page is the furniture leaving the room. It is not a request to saw a leg, unscrew a wall-mounted board, or clear a whole bedroom you have not listed.

If the visit is only this board and nothing else that counts as furniture, say that. A single awkward piece can still be named on its own. You can add it to [a one-off pickup](/services/junk-removal) when the rest of the visit is mixed household junk and the board is one line in that mix. That page is not a bin, and it is not a request to hide the board in a sack.

Say, in one message:

- one ironing board, and which door it was behind
- standing free, or “fixed to the wall, staying” if you found screws and left them
- cover off or cover left on, and that the edge was checked
- folded flat, or “legs stay open”, or “no fold”
- the length and width as it will travel, or “not measured”
- the iron, if it is leaving, as its own line
- anything else from that gap that is leaving, as its own line

Do not write “bedroom” as the only word. Do not write “it can go in a bag”. Do not write a length you did not measure. Do not ask for a leg to be cut because the folded board is longer than the door is wide. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If the board is still behind the door and you have not looked under the cover, you are early. Move it out, check the cover, try the lever. If the lever will not fold the legs, the open measurement is the honest one. Send that.

On the morning, leave the board where the photos left it, folded or open as you described. Check the cover has not been filled again overnight. Open the door to the gap you measured after the board was moved. A shoe kicked into that gap changes the width. Move it before you treat the old figure as current.

Point at the top and the legs as the board. Point at the iron only if it is the separate line. Point at the wardrobe, the bed or the bin only to show that they are not this object. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: one ironing board, out from behind a named door, cover checked, fold tried, and the legs either flat or still open.
