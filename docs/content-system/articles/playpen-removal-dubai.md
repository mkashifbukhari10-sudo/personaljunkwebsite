The item is one playpen, and the fold has stopped working. Do not force it. Empty it, find out which kind of playpen it is, and write whether it folds, half folds, or stays standing. A playpen that will not fold is still a playpen. The booking only needs to know which shape it will leave in.

## Empty it before you touch the fold

Take everything out of the playpen. Toys, blankets, soft blocks, books, a play mat, bottles, a dummy that rolled into a corner. Lift the floor pad if there is one and look under it. Small things live under the pad.

Check the pockets. Many mesh playpens have a storage pocket on the outside, or a changing shelf that clips on top. Empty those too. Remove any clip-on toy bar, mobile or activity panel that comes off by hand, and keep it with the playpen if it is leaving. Write “toy bar off, with playpen” or “no toy bar”.

If the toys are also leaving, they are a separate line. Bag them, and write what is in the bag in plain words: “soft toys, one bag”, “plastic blocks, one box”. Do not leave them in the playpen so they leave together. A playpen full of toys looks like one item and is not.

If the floor of the playpen is a foam mat, or a set of interlocking foam tiles, take it out and stack it. Foam tiles take more space in a vehicle than their weight suggests. Count them, or write “one stack of foam tiles, about knee high”. Do not roll a foam mat tight with tape to make it look smaller and then describe it as small.

Write “playpen emptied, pad checked” when it is done.

## Which kind of playpen it is

Three kinds are common. They fold in different ways, so they fail in different ways. Write which one you have.

A mesh playpen. Four mesh sides on a metal or plastic frame, with a top rail on each side and a floor. It usually folds by unlocking the top rails, then pulling up a strap or a hub in the middle of the floor so the frame closes like an umbrella. Some come with a carry bag.

A panel playpen. Several plastic or wooden panels joined by hinges or pins, standing in a ring or a square. There is often no floor. It folds by unhinging the panels, or by folding them flat against each other like a screen. Some sets include a panel with a small gate in it.

A wooden playpen with slatted sides. A square of four wooden sides with bars, often with a floor board and sometimes with a base that can be raised or lowered. It usually comes apart at the corners with bolts or pins rather than folding.

Write the kind, the material and the size you can see. “Mesh playpen, grey, square, with a floor” is a description. “Baby thing” is not.

If you have the manual, or the model name is printed on a label, write the name. If you do not, write “no label”. Do not search for a manual and then describe a fold you have not tried. The fold you can make by hand, today, is the fact.

## Find where the fold stops

Try the fold the way you normally would, once, gently. Then stop and write where it stopped. These are the usual places.

- A top rail that will not unlock: on a mesh playpen, each top rail usually has a release in the middle of the rail, under the fabric. If one rail stays locked, the frame cannot close. Write “one rail will not unlock” and say which side as you face it.
- A centre hub that will not pull up: the strap is there but the floor does not rise, or it rises and stops. Write “hub stuck” and say how far it moved.
- A hinge that will not turn: on a panel playpen, a hinge or a pin between two panels may be stiff, bent or missing. Write “hinge stiff between panels three and four”, counting from the gate panel or from any panel you choose, as long as you say which.
- A pin that is missing: a panel set may have lost the pin that joins two panels. Then the ring is already open at that point. Write “pin missing, ring open”.
- A bolt that is seized: on a wooden playpen, a corner bolt may be painted over or rusted. Write “corner bolt seized”.

Do not stand on a rail to make it unlock. Do not hit a hub with your foot. Do not bend a top rail past the point where it moves easily. A mesh rail that is bent hard can crack and leave a sharp edge inside the fabric. A forced hinge on a plastic panel can snap and leave a spike. A playpen that will not fold is a size problem. A playpen that has been forced is a sharp-edge problem as well.

If the fold works on a second gentle try, after you have found the release, you may fold it. Write “folded, by hand”. If you need a tool you do not already use on it, leave it standing and write “not folded, left standing”. Both are acceptable. Only one of them is true for your playpen.

If it half folds, describe the half. “Two sides folded in, two still locked, about half the width” tells the person reading the booking what will come through the door. “Partly folded” does not.

## Panels that already come apart

A panel playpen often comes apart without folding. The panels lift off their pins, or slide out of their hinges, one by one. If that is how you set it up, it is how it comes down.

If the panels lift apart by hand, and you already do that when you move it, you may take them apart. Stack them flat, face to face, and count them. Write “eight panels, stacked, gate panel included”. Tie the stack with a strap or string if they slide. Put the pins, clips and any loose feet in one bag and tape the bag to the top panel.

If one hinge is stuck and the rest come apart, leave the stuck pair joined. Write “six panels loose, two still joined”. Do not force the last hinge to make the stack even.

A wooden playpen whose corners are held with bolts is a different job. The bolts are the work. If the bolts turn easily with the key that came with it, and you already used that key to put it together, you may take the sides off. Write “four sides off, bolts bagged”. If they do not turn, leave it standing. The decision on furniture built to come apart, where the screws are the job, stays on the [dismantling guide](/blog/furniture-dismantling-dubai).

Do not saw a wooden side. Do not cut the mesh off a frame to make it lighter. Do not pull a fabric skin off a metal frame. The playpen leaves as a playpen, folded, in panels, or standing. The fabric and the frame stay together.

## Measure it in the shape it will leave in

If it folded, measure it folded. Length, width and thickness. A mesh playpen folded into a long bundle is easy to carry, and its length is the number that matters.

If it did not fold, measure it standing. The width of each side, the height of the top rail, and the diagonal from one corner to the opposite corner at the top. The diagonal is the size it will be when it is tilted to pass a door.

If it is in panels, measure one panel and count them. Write the height of the stack.

Then measure the door it has to leave through. Open the door as far as it goes. Measure the clear gap from the frame to the door edge, not the width of the door leaf. If the playpen is in a bedroom, the bedroom door is often the tightest. If it is on a balcony, the balcony door is often a sliding door with a track and a frame that reduce the clear width.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a playpen, and it does not keep a standard size for a door.

A square mesh playpen that will not fold is often wider than an interior door. Write it if that is what you see: “standing playpen is wider than the bedroom door”. Then look at whether it can be tilted on its side through the door. A mesh side is soft, but the top rails are not. Do not squeeze the mesh through and assume the rails will follow.

## Where it is standing

Write the room. Living room, child’s room, a corner of the bedroom, a balcony, or a covered garden area in a villa. “Living room, ground floor of the villa” is a location. “Downstairs” is not.

A playpen on a balcony has been in the sun and in the dust. Look at the plastic for cracks and fading. Look at the metal joints for rust. Write “sun-faded”, “cracked rail” or “rust on hinge” if you see it. Do not describe a balcony playpen as “like new” because the fabric looks clean from the front.

A playpen in a villa garden may be standing on grass or tiles. Write which. If it has been pegged into the ground, write “pegged” and pull the pegs only if they come out by hand. Bag the pegs with the playpen. If it is standing on soft ground and has sunk, write “sunk into grass”. A playpen that has to be lifted out of wet soil is not the clean piece in your photo.

If the playpen is in a room the child still uses, move it out of the walkway only when you are ready to empty and fold it. Do not leave a half-folded frame where a child can climb on it or close a finger in a hinge. If it cannot be folded and the child is still in the home, leave it standing and empty until the visit.

## Who decides that it goes

A playpen is often borrowed, handed down, or bought by a grandparent. Ask before it leaves. A playpen that belongs to someone else is not yours to send away because it will not fold.

In a furnished rental, a playpen is unlikely to be on the inventory, but check if the flat came with nursery furniture. In a short-let flat or a holiday home, a playpen may belong to the operator. The [short-let clearance note](/blog/short-let-furnished-flat-clearance-dubai) covers furniture a manager is clearing from a flat they run. This note does not interpret that inventory.

If the playpen is still usable and someone wants it, the [old-furniture disposal guide](/blog/dispose-old-furniture-dubai) sets out the choice between passing an item on and having it collected. A playpen with a stuck fold, a split rail or a missing pin is not ready for another child. Do not describe it as ready to pass on because it looks clean. Write the fault, and let the person deciding read it.

## What is not this playpen

A cot is not this playpen. A cot has a sleeping base, a mattress, and sides a child sleeps inside. A cot is not given a page of its own here. Name it on the children’s room list if it is leaving.

A travel cot is the hard case, because many mesh playpens are sold as travel cots and used as both. Write what yours was used for. If it held a mattress and a child slept in it, write “travel cot, also used as playpen”. If it was only a play space on the floor, write “playpen”. Do not drop the word cot to fit this page, and do not drop the mattress out of the description. A cot mattress is its own line. The [mattress guide](/blog/mattress-disposal-dubai) keeps mattresses.

A safety gate is not a playpen. A gate that presses into a doorway or a stair opening, or that is screwed into the wall, is a gate. A gate screwed into the wall is a fixture, and the holes are a decision for the owner of the wall. Some panel playpens can be opened into a long barrier and used as a gate across a room. If yours is a panel set used that way, it is still this playpen. Write “panel playpen, set up as a barrier”.

A play tent, a ball pit, a slide, a playhouse or a ride-on toy is not a playpen. Each is its own line on the children’s room list. Large plastic play items are the kind of thing that guide asks you to show in the photos.

A garden trampoline with a net enclosure is not a playpen, however much it looks like one from a window. It has its own [trampoline guide](/blog/trampoline-removal-dubai). A dog pen made of metal panels is not a child’s playpen. Write what it is used for.

## One line in the booking

You can [include it in the pickup](/services/junk-removal) once it is empty, the kind is named, and the note says how far it folds. That page is a single collection or a mixed household load. It is not a request to force a fold, to cut a frame, or to take a gate off a wall.

If the playpen is leaving with a high chair, a cot and a chest of drawers from the same room, the [furniture removal](/services/furniture-removal) page fits the larger load. Name the playpen as its own line on that list. Do not hide it inside “nursery furniture”.

Say, in one message:

- one playpen, and its kind: mesh, panel or wooden
- emptied, with the pad checked
- where the fold stops, if it stops
- folded by hand, half folded, in panels, or standing
- the size in that shape, and the clear width of the door it has to pass, or “not measured”
- a crack, a split rail, rust or a missing pin, if you can see it
- the room and the floor, or the balcony or garden
- anything that is not this playpen: toys, foam tiles, a cot, a cot mattress, a gate

Do not write “baby stuff” as the only words. Do not write “folds flat” if one rail is still locked. Do not ask for the frame to be cut because it will not fold. The fold and the door are the report.

If you have two playpens, write two lines. Say which one folds and which does not. Two playpens of the same kind are still two items, each with its own fold.
