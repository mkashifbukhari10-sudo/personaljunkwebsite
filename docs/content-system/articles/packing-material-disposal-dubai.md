After a move, sort the packaging before arranging a pickup. Flatten clean, dry cartons for the building's permitted recycling route where one is available. Keep foam, plastic film, damaged cardboard and any wooden packaging separate. If the volume is too large for the route available to you, photograph the piles and ask for a quote for [junk removal](/services/junk-removal). A removal booking can also cover unwanted household items, but list those separately from packaging.

## Find out what the movers will collect

Before dismantling every box, check the mover's agreement. Some boxes or protective materials may belong to the moving company or be due for collection. Keep those separate from material you own. If you are still unpacking, choose a date after the essential rooms are finished so the final quantity is visible. Booking too early leaves a second pile to deal with later.

Keep one small stock of sound cartons only if you have a real use and a place to store them. Do not preserve every box on the chance that it might be useful. Flatten the rest as you unpack. Remove loose tape where practical, bundle sheets of cardboard so they can be carried, and keep the stack dry. Wet cardboard collapses and is harder to handle through a corridor or lift.

## Divide the pile by material

Cardboard cartons and paper wrapping can be grouped together if they are clean and dry. Plastic stretch wrap, bubble wrap, foam sheets and molded inserts should form separate piles. Ask your building or collection provider which of these materials its route accepts; a recycling label on a product does not prove that every local collection stream takes it.

Wooden crates and pallets need their own description. They can have protruding nails, broken boards or heavy bases. Do not hide them beneath flattened cartons. Photograph their size and condition before requesting collection. If a crate held an appliance or fragile item, check that no parts, manuals or accessories are still inside it.

Put sharp or broken packing pieces in a safe container and tell the collector what it contains. Keep batteries, aerosols, paint, cleaning products and electronic devices out of the packaging pile. Those materials need an answer of their own, as the [acceptance guide](/blog/what-we-take-dubai) explains.

## Use the route that fits the actual volume

For a few dry cartons, use the recycling or waste facilities your building provides, following its posted rules. Do not fill a shared bin room with a house move's worth of boxes without checking capacity and permission. Large piles may need a booked removal even though each individual box is light. They take up floor space, obstruct access and can fill a vehicle before the heavy items are loaded.

If the packaging is mixed with an old bed, broken shelving or furniture that did not fit the new home, show the complete load in the quote request. One wide photo of every pile is more useful than a close view of a single carton. Include the floor, lift booking, parking and distance to the loading point. The guide to [estimating a load](/blog/how-much-fits-in-one-load-dubai) explains why flattened cartons and intact boxes occupy very different amounts of space.

## Keep the common route clear

Store the sorted piles inside your home or another space you are permitted to use. A corridor, fire exit or lift lobby is not a waiting area for packaging. If the pickup must use a service lift or loading bay, ask management for the applicable process and book the slot before confirming the collection time. See the [service-lift guide](/blog/service-lift-booking-dubai) for the access details to gather.

On collection day, keep the moving company's reusable materials apart from the disposal pile. Walk the rooms once more before the crew leaves: behind doors, under the bed and in the kitchen are easy places to miss a carton. The final check prevents an extra trip for the last stack.
