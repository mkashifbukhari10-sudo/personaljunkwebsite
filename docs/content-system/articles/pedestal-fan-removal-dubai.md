## Make sure it is a pedestal fan

A pedestal fan, for this note, stands on the floor. It has a base, a pole, and a head with a guard and blades. The head may tilt. The pole may be meant to slide shorter. The base may be a round weight, a cross, or a tripod. Write which one you have. “Round base, pole will not slide, head will not tilt down.” “Tripod base, one leg stuck open.”

A desk fan is not this fan. It sits on a table and has no floor pole. If that is the object, write “desk fan” and keep it off this note. It can leave in the same visit only as its own line. Do not call it a pedestal because you are tired of naming things.

A tower fan is a tall box with no round head. Name it as a tower if that is what it is. This note is the fan with a head, a guard and a pole. Do not saw a tower to make it match these steps.

A fan fixed to the ceiling is not this fan. That job is unconfirmed here. This page does not describe how to take one down, and it does not tell you to remove blades, a canopy or a wire. If the fan is on the ceiling, write “ceiling fan, not this page” and stop. Do not book it by calling it a pedestal fan that will not fold.

A fan screwed to the wall is a fitting. Leave it on the wall. Write “wall fan, staying”. Do not unscrew it under these steps.

A floor lamp is not a fan. A lamp has a shade and no blades. The [floor-lamp note](/blog/floor-lamp-taller-than-doorway-dubai) is that lamp, measured against a doorway. Do not describe a fan as a lamp because both are tall, and do not describe a lamp as a fan because both stand on the floor.

A vacuum cleaner is not a fan. It picks up dust and may have a hose. The [vacuum note](/blog/vacuum-cleaner-removal-dubai) is that machine. A fan does not have a dust compartment. Do not empty a vacuum under these steps, and do not put a fan in the vacuum’s cupboard line.

If you are not sure, write the words on the label and “not sure it is a pedestal fan”. A label you can read is better than a guess.

## Unplug it before you fold anything

Switch the fan off at its own switch, then at the wall if it is plugged in. Unplug the wall end. If the lead detaches from the fan, unplug that end too and keep it with the fan. Coil the cord loosely. Use a clip the fan already has. Do not wrap the cord tightly around the guard, and do not tape it across a cracked housing.

If the plug is behind a sofa you are keeping, and you cannot reach the socket without dragging the sofa onto the cord, do not drag it. Write “plug not seen” and stop trying to fold the fan while it may still be live. Move what blocks your hand. If you still cannot see the plug, leave the fan standing and say so.

Some fans have a cord that does not come off. That cord stays with the fan. Write “cord fixed, coiled”.

A remote control is a small extra, not the fan. If it is leaving, tape it to the pole in one piece of tape that does not cover a crack, or put it in a small bag with the fan. Write “remote with fan” or “remote kept”. A remote you will use with a replacement is not spare because this fan is leaving.

Do not cut the cord to make the fan shorter. A cut cord is a bare end, not a folded fan.

## The joint that will not close

What makes this fan a problem is the joint. A pedestal fan is sold as something that packs down. The neck tilts, the pole slides into itself, or the legs fold. When that movement has stopped, the fan is as tall and as wide as it stands, and forcing it makes a bent pole or a cracked neck.

Look at one joint at a time.

The neck is the hinge under the head. If it already tilts by the knob or the squeeze you use when you aim the breeze, you may tilt the head down and write “head tilted down”. If it does not move, write “neck stuck”. Do not hit the head. Do not stand on the base and lean on the guard. A stuck neck stays stuck.

The pole is the tube. If it already shortens by a collar you loosen by hand, loosen that collar, slide the pole down, and tighten the collar again so it does not drop on a foot later. Write “pole shortened”. If the collar will not turn, or the tube will not slide, write “pole stuck at full height”. Do not oil it as a repair. Do not hammer the collar. Do not saw the tube. A shorter fan made with a saw is a sharp edge and a different object.

The base is the part on the floor. A round base does not fold. Measure it as it is. A cross base does not fold. A tripod folds only if the legs already close at hinges you can move by hand. If they close, close them, stand the fan so it cannot fall, and write “legs closed”. If one leg stays out, write “one leg stuck open” and leave the others as they are. Do not bend a leg to match the ones that moved.

A height-adjust knob that spins without catching is a knob that has failed. Write “knob turns, pole does not move”. Do not keep turning it until the plastic splits.

If you have already forced a joint and it is bent, stop. Write “pole bent” or “neck cracked” and do not bend it back. The photo has to show the shape the fan actually has.

## Guard, blades and the base weight

The guard is the cage around the blades. Leave it on. A guard that is clipped the way you already open it for cleaning may come off if you are keeping the fan in parts you can name. For a fan that is leaving, the guard is safer on the head. Write “guard on”. Do not remove the blades. Do not reach through the guard. A loose blade is a cut, not a smaller fan.

If the guard is already split, or a blade is already loose and you can see it without opening anything, write “guard split” or “blade loose, guard left on”. Do not pull the blade out to tidy the photo.

The base is often the heavy part. A round base can be sand, concrete or a metal plate. Do not open it. Do not tip the fan to pour anything out. If the base is cracked and grit is already on the floor, write “base cracked, grit on the floor” and do not sweep that grit into a sack with the fan. Sweep it up as ordinary dust if it is ordinary dust, and leave the fan standing.

A fan that wobbles because a foot is missing is still one fan. Write “one foot missing”. Do not unscrew the other feet to make it look even.

Oscillation is the head turning from side to side. If a knob for that is already off, leave it off. If it is on and the head is stuck mid-turn, do not force the head to the centre. Write “head stuck to one side”.

## Where the fan is standing

The fan is awkward because of where it was left, as well as because of the fold.

In a corner it may be behind a curtain, a drying rack or a chair. Move what is yours to move, and put those things where they are not the fan. If a drying rack is also leaving, it is its own line. The [drying-rack note](/blog/drying-rack-removal-dubai) is that rack. The balcony guide already counts racks and old fans together when the balcony is the job. This note is the one fan whose fold has stopped. Do not call the corner “the fan”.

On a balcony, the base may be rusty and the cord may run under a door. Unplug it inside before you lift it. If the cord is pinched in the door, free the cord. Do not shut the door on it to “hold it”. If the fan is tied to a railing with a strap or a wire, untie the strap if it is a loose strap. If the fan is bolted or screwed to the railing or the floor, write “fixed to the balcony” and do not unscrew the building. This page starts when the fan already stands free. A fan that is part of the balcony fitting is not this object.

In a cupboard, the pole may be caught on a shelf. Unhook it. Do not yank the fan against a cord looped over a bracket. If it will not come out without taking the cupboard apart, write “stuck in the cupboard” and photograph it there. Do not dismantle the cupboard. The cupboard stays with the flat unless it is a freestanding piece you have already decided is leaving, and that piece is not this note.

Beside a bed, the fan may be the thing you used all summer. It is still one fan. Do not pack it inside a bedside table, a wardrobe or a box of bedding. A pair of tables is a different note. A whole bedroom is a different note. The fan stays its own line.

## One fan, not the room

A second fan is a second line, with its own plug and its own fold. Do not strap two fans together. Do not put a desk fan inside the guard of the pedestal fan.

A box of small heaters, irons and desk fans, leaving as part of a wider clear-out, belongs on the acceptance list as that wider load. This note stays the one pedestal fan that will not fold. You can still use these steps on each pedestal fan in a larger load, one at a time, so each one is unplugged and the stuck joint is named. The list of what else the load may include stays on the acceptance page.

If several unlike appliances are on the same booking, a fridge, a washer and this fan, the old-appliance guide is the register. Keep this fan off that register’s white-goods line. It is still one line of its own, prepared here.

Do not put tools, a remote, or kitchen rubbish inside the guard. The guard stays closed.

A fan you are sending to another room in the same home, still yours, is not a collection. This note is for the fan that is leaving the property. If someone else wants it and it works, arrange that handover yourself. Keep it out of the leaving photos until they have collected it. A message that says “I might take it” is not a handover.

The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a fan is worth.

## Measure the fan as it will travel

Measure after you have tried the joints, not before, and not with a guess.

Height from the floor to the top of the guard, with the head in the position it will stay in. Width of the guard. Width of the base, including a leg that is stuck open. If the pole was shortened, measure the new height. If it was not, measure the height you have.

Then look at the opening it has to pass. The useful figures are the clear height and clear width of that door with the door as open as it goes. A door closer, a shoe or a curtain can change the gap. Move what you are keeping, then measure if the gap changed. If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a fan or for a door.

Write one sentence you measured. “Pole stuck at full height, guard wider than the balcony door” or “pole shortened, base still wider than the cupboard door” or “not measured”. Do not write “it will fit” if you have not compared the figures. Do not write “it will not fit” from memory of a different door.

Weight is not something to guess. A base you cannot lift alone is “base heavy”. Do not write a number you did not weigh.

The wider method for one object, photographs and the narrowest point on the way out, stays with the [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) if the corridor and the lift are the question. Stay on this page for the fold, the guard and the base. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether the pole was shortened.

## One line in the booking

You can [include the fan in the pickup](/services/junk-removal) once it is unplugged or marked unseen, and the joint that will not move has been named. That page is a single pickup or a mixed household load. It is not a request to saw a pole, open a base, or take a fan down from a ceiling.

If the fan is one of several machines, say so, and use the old-appliance guide for the others. The fan remains its own line.

Say, in one message:

- one pedestal fan, and where it stands
- unplugged, cord coiled, or “plug not seen”
- neck stuck, head tilted down, pole shortened, or “pole stuck at full height”
- legs closed, or “one leg stuck open”, if the base is a tripod
- guard on, and any split or loose blade
- the height and the widths you measured, or “not measured”
- anything else leaving from the same corner, as its own line
- that it is not a ceiling fan, not a wall fan, and not a floor lamp

Do not write “fan” as the only word if the ceiling also has a fan you are not sending. Do not write “it folds” if you have not closed the joint. Do not ask for the pole to be cut because the figure is larger than the gap. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not tried the collar or the neck, you are early. Try the movement the fan already has. If it will not move, the stuck measurement is the honest one. Send that.

On the morning, stand the fan where the photos stood it. Check the plug is still out. Check the guard is still on. A curtain dropped across the fan is not a fold. Move it before you treat the old figure as current.

Point at the base, the pole and the head as the fan. Point at a ceiling fan only to show that it is staying. Point at a drying rack or a second fan only if that object is its own line. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: one freestanding pedestal fan, unplugged or unseen, and the joint that will not close written in plain words.
