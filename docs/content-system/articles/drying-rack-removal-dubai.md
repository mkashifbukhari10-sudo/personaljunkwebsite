The item is one drying rack from a balcony. Empty it, try the fold it already has, and say whether it stands free or is still tied to the railing. That is the job. A balcony full of buckets, fans and old pots is not. An ironing board behind a bedroom door is not.

This note is one rack. A mixed balcony, with several light pieces counted together, is the [balcony guide](/blog/balcony-clearance-dubai). What a household load can include, when the rack rides with other things, is the [acceptance list](/blog/what-we-take-dubai). Come back here when the question is one drying rack, the fold, and the railing or the balcony door.

## Make sure it is a drying rack

A drying rack, for this note, is a freestanding frame made to hold wet clothes. It may be a pair of wings that open on a hinge. It may be an X-frame that scissor-opens. It may be a tower of tiers that is meant to collapse. It may be a single rail on a tripod. Write which one you have. “Two wings, one hinge stuck open.” “X-frame, will not close.” “Three tiers, the bottom tier will not drop.”

An ironing board is not this rack. A board has a long top for an iron, and a cover. The [ironing-board note](/blog/ironing-board-removal-dubai) is that board, the cover and the door it was blocking. Do not call a rack a board because both fold, and do not call a board a rack because both stand in a gap.

A pedestal fan is not a rack. A fan has a guard, blades and a pole. The [pedestal-fan note](/blog/pedestal-fan-removal-dubai) is that fan. A rack does not plug in unless it is a heated airer, and even then it is not a fan. Do not describe a fan as a rack because both were left on the balcony.

A clothes line that is screwed to the wall, or a pulley line fixed between two walls, is a fitting. Leave it. Write “fixed line, staying”. Do not unscrew the wall, and do not cut a wire that is part of the building. This note starts when the rack already stands on the floor, or when it is only tied with a loose strap you can untie.

A heated towel rail that is plumbed or screwed to a bathroom wall is a fitting. Leave it. Write “towel rail, staying”. Do not disconnect a pipe under these steps.

A washer or a dryer is not a rack. If the machine is leaving, it is its own line, with its own disconnection. Do not strap a rack to a machine to make one item.

Clothes are not the rack. A shirt on a bar is laundry. Take it off before you describe the frame. If the clothes are also leaving, they are their own line. Do not call a pile of wet washing “the drying rack”.

If you are not sure, write the shape you can see. “Metal frame, two wings, no ironing top” is enough. A wrong name is worse than a plain description.

## Empty it before you fold it

Take every garment off the bars. Shake the pegs into a bag if they are leaving with the rack, or put them in a drawer if you are keeping them. Write “pegs with rack” or “pegs kept”. A peg is not a reason to leave the rack open.

Look for a sheet, a towel or a cover draped over the frame to hide it. Lift that off. It may be hiding a broken hinge, a sharp end, or a second smaller rack nested inside. Write what you found. Do not fold the cover into the frame and then forget it.

Check the feet and the joints for socks, a belt, a clothes hanger, or a piece of string. A hanger caught in a hinge will stop the fold and then snap. Take it out.

If the clothes are still wet, they are still not the rack. Hang them where you are keeping them, or bag them as their own line if they are leaving. Do not roll wet clothes into the frame to make the photo look tidy. Wet fabric against a metal hinge is a stain on the floor when the rack is tipped, not a smaller load.

A laundry basket standing beside the rack is not the rack. If the basket is leaving, it is its own line. If it is staying, move it so the photo shows the rack. Do not put the rack inside the basket and call that one item. A rack that does not fit in the basket is still a rack.

An iron left on the balcony beside the rack is not part of the rack. If the iron is leaving, it is a small appliance on its own line. This page does not empty an iron and does not open a steam tank. Write “iron, separate” or “iron kept”.

## The joint that will not close

What makes this rack a problem is the joint. A drying rack is sold as something that folds flat. When the hinge, the scissor or the tier has stopped, the rack is as wide as it stands, and forcing it bends a bar or snaps a plastic knuckle.

Look at one joint at a time.

A wing rack opens like a book. If both wings already close by the movement you use when you put it away, close them and write “wings closed”. If one wing will not meet the other, write “one wing stuck open”. Do not stand on the lower bar. Do not lean your weight on the top rail. A stuck wing stays stuck.

An X-frame closes by bringing the feet together. If it already does that by hand, close it and hold it with the strap or the clip the rack already has. Write “X-frame closed”. If the feet will not come in, write “X-frame stuck open”. Do not saw a bar to make the X smaller. A cut bar is a sharp end, not a folded rack.

A tower has tiers. If a tier already drops when you release the catch it was built with, drop that tier and write “tiers collapsed”. If a catch will not move, leave that tier where it is and write “one tier stuck”. Do not remove the bars to make a shorter tower. The bars are the rack.

A tripod of legs closes only if the legs already fold at hinges you can move by hand. If they close, close them and write “legs closed”. If one leg stays out, write “one leg stuck open”. Do not bend a leg to match the others.

A plastic joint that has already cracked gets the words “joint cracked”. Do not glue it as a repair for the booking, and do not pull the cracked piece off so the photo looks clean. A loose piece of plastic that has already come off goes in a bag with the rack. Write “broken knuckle, bagged with rack”.

If you have already forced a joint and a bar is bent, stop. Write “bar bent” and do not bend it back. The photo has to show the shape the rack actually has.

A heated airer is still this note when it is freestanding and the question is the fold. Unplug it first. If the plug is behind a door you cannot open without dragging the rack onto the cord, write “plug not seen” and do not fold it while it may still be live. Do not cut the cord to make the frame shorter. A heated rail that is fixed to the wall is not this note. That line was already written as staying.

## Tied to the railing, or standing free

On a balcony the rack is often tied so the wind does not take it. The tie decides whether this page can start.

A loose strap, a bit of rope, or a cable tie you can cut without touching a fixing, can come off. Untie the strap. Cut a cable tie with scissors if you have them, and take the cut piece with the rack so it is not left on the floor. Write “strap off” or “cable tie cut, rack free”.

A chain and a padlock are not a loose strap. If you have the key, unlock it and keep the lock if it is yours. Write “lock off”. If you do not have the key, write “locked to the railing” and stop. Do not cut a chain that is locked, and do not ask for the railing to be cut.

A bolt, a screw or a bracket into the railing, the floor or the wall means the rack is fixed. Write “fixed to the balcony” and do not unscrew the building. This page starts when the rack already stands free. A frame that is part of the balcony is not this object.

Wire wrapped many times around a bar and a railing can be unwound if it is loose wire and you can do it by hand without cutting into the railing. If the wire is tight and you cannot see the end, write “wire tight, not freed” and leave it. Do not use a grinder, and do not pull until the railing moves.

Rust on the feet is not a fixing. A rusty rack that stands on its own feet is free. Write “feet rusty, standing free”. Do not tip it over the balcony edge to knock the rust off. Do not hose it as a preparation step. Water on a balcony runs somewhere, and a wet floor inside the flat is a separate problem.

If the rack has fallen and is lying against the railing, stand it only if it will stand. If it will not, leave it lying and write “lying, will not stand”. Do not hang it back on the railing to make the photo match an older picture.

## The balcony door

The rack is often the thing that stops the balcony door. The door then will not shut, or it will not open past the frame. That is part of this note. A rack that already stands folded in a corner, with the door clear, is a smaller job. Say which one you have.

Open the door as far as it will go without forcing the rack. If the door hits a wing, stop. Do not keep pushing. A sliding door that is off its track is a building problem, not a fold. Write “door hits the rack” or “door clear”.

A rack that lives indoors, behind a bedroom door or in a laundry gap, is still this note when it is one drying rack and the question is the fold. Say where it stands. “Behind the bedroom door” is a place. It does not turn the rack into an ironing board. The ironing-board note stays the board. If both are in that gap, each is its own line.

Move what else is in the gap. A mop, a bucket, a pair of shoes, a folded chair. Put those where they are not the rack. If a chair is leaving, it is its own line. Do not call the gap “the drying rack”.

A curtain caught on a wing comes off the wing. Do not cut the curtain. If the curtain is staying, leave it on its rail and free the rack.

## One rack, not the balcony

A second rack is a second line, with its own fold and its own tie. Do not strap two open racks together. Do not nest them unless they already nest by the movement they were built with, and then write “two racks, nested” only if that is true. If they will not nest, say so and leave them separate.

The balcony guide already counts drying racks with umbrellas, mops, buckets and fans when the balcony is the job. Stay on that guide when the load is the mix. Stay on this page when one rack is the question, even if it was standing on that balcony. You can still use these steps on each rack in a larger load, one at a time, so each one is empty and the stuck joint is named. The list of what else the balcony holds stays on the balcony page.

A box of pegs, a basket of clothes and a broken umbrella, leaving with the rack as part of a wider clear-out, belong on the acceptance list as that wider load. This note stays the one rack. Do not hide the umbrella inside the frame.

Do not put tools, a bottle of detergent, or kitchen rubbish between the wings. The wings stay empty.

A rack you are moving to another balcony in the same home, still yours, is not a collection. This note is for the rack that is leaving the property. If someone else wants it and it still folds, arrange that handover yourself. Keep it out of the leaving photos until they have collected it. A message that says “I might take it” is not a handover.

The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a rack is worth.

A ceiling fan, a wall fan, and a fan fixed to the balcony are not this page. A ceiling fan is unconfirmed here. Do not book a fan by calling it a drying rack.

## Measure the rack as it will travel

Measure after you have tried the fold, not before, and not with a guess.

If the wings closed, measure the folded thickness, the height and the length. If a wing stayed open, measure the width as it stands, including the stuck wing. If the X-frame closed, measure the closed bundle. If it did not, measure the spread of the feet.

Then look at the opening it has to pass. For a balcony rack, that opening is often the balcony door, then a room door, then the front door. The useful figures are the clear height and clear width of the narrowest of those, with the door as open as it goes. A door closer, a shoe or a track can change the gap. Move what you are keeping, then measure if the gap changed.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a rack or for a balcony door.

Write one sentence you measured. “Wings closed, folded rack longer than the balcony door is wide” or “one wing stuck, wider than the bedroom door” or “not measured”. Do not write “it will fit” if you have not compared the figures. Do not write “it will not fit” from memory of a different door.

Weight is not something to guess. A tower you cannot lift alone is “heavy”. Do not write a number you did not weigh.

The wider method for one object, photographs and the narrowest point on the way out, stays with the [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) if the corridor and the lift are the question. Stay on this page for the fold, the tie and the balcony door. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether the wings closed.

## One line in the booking

You can book [a balcony rubbish pickup](/services/junk-removal) once the rack is empty, the fold has been tried, and the note says whether it stands free. That page is a single pickup or a mixed household load. It is not a request to saw a bar, cut a locked chain, or unscrew a railing.

If the rack is leaving with chairs or another freestanding piece, you can [take the rack with the other furniture](/services/furniture-removal). That page is the furniture leaving the home. It is not a new name for this rack, and it is not a reason to fold the rack into a wardrobe note. The rack remains its own line.

Say, in one message:

- one drying rack, and where it stands
- wings closed, X-frame closed, tiers collapsed, or the joint that will not move
- standing free, strap off, or “fixed to the balcony”
- empty of clothes, and where the pegs went
- the height and the widths you measured, or “not measured”
- anything else leaving from the same balcony, as its own line
- that it is not an ironing board, not a pedestal fan, and not a fixed clothes line

Do not write “balcony” as the only word if the rack is one piece and the rest is staying. Do not write “it folds” if you have not closed the joint. Do not ask for a bar to be cut because the figure is larger than the gap. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not tried the hinge, you are early. Try the movement the rack already has. If it will not move, the stuck measurement is the honest one. Send that.

On the morning, stand the rack where the photos stood it. Check it is still empty. Check a strap you removed has not been tied back. A towel dropped over the frame is not a fold. Take it off before you treat the old figure as current.

Point at the frame as the rack. Point at a fan, a board or a basket only if that object is its own line. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: one drying rack, emptied, the fold tried, and standing free or still tied, written in plain words.
