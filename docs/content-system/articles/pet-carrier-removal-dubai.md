## Make sure it is a plastic pet carrier

A plastic pet carrier, for this note, is a hard box with a door, vents, and a shape an animal was meant to travel in. The door may be a metal grille, a plastic flap, or a wire panel on plastic clips. The top may lift off. The two halves may be meant to come apart at clips. Write which one you have. “Top-opening, grille door, latch stuck.” “Two halves, clips will not release.” “Front door only, no separate top.”

A soft fabric carrier, a sling or a tote with mesh sides, is not this box. Write “soft carrier” and keep it off these steps. It can leave in the same visit only as its own line. Do not call it plastic because you want one note for every pet bag in the store.

A wire crate with no plastic shell is not this carrier. Write “wire crate” and leave it as that line. Do not bend the wire to make it match a plastic box, and do not follow the door steps below as if a clip on a wire door were a plastic latch.

A stroller is not a carrier. A stroller has wheels, a handle and a seat. The [stroller note](/blog/stroller-removal-dubai) is that buggy. Do not describe a carrier as a stroller because both were used for a small animal or a child, and do not describe a stroller as a carrier because both fold or both have a basket.

A playpen is not a carrier. A playpen stands on the floor and is meant to hold a child for a while, not to be carried as a closed box. The [playpen note](/blog/playpen-removal-dubai) is that fold. Do not collapse a playpen under these steps.

A suitcase is not a carrier. A suitcase has a shell for clothes and a zip or a lock, and no animal door. If the object is a suitcase, name it as a suitcase. This page does not empty a case of clothes and call it a carrier.

A car seat is not a carrier. It has a harness for a child and fittings for a car. A car seat is not given a page of its own here. Write “car seat” and stop following these steps.

A plastic storage box with a lid is not a carrier, even if a pet once slept in it. If it is a crate left after an unpack, that is the [plastic-crate note](/blog/plastic-crates-after-unpacking-dubai). This page is the box that was built as a carrier, with a door rather than only a lid.

If an animal is inside, this note does not start. Take the animal out yourself, to somewhere you have already decided. Do not book a collection of an occupied carrier. Do not ask a crew to handle an animal. This page is the empty box, after the animal is gone. If you cannot remove the animal, stop and write “animal still inside”. That is not a carrier ready to leave.

If you are not sure, write “hard plastic box, door on the front, not sure it is a carrier”. A label you can read is better than a guess.

## Empty it before you try the door

Take out the bedding, the newspaper, the pad, the bowl, and any toy. A bowl clipped to the door comes off if it already unclips. If it will not unclip, leave it and write “bowl still clipped”. Do not smash the bowl off the grille.

Look under the pad. Keys, a collar, a lead, a packet of food, a small bottle. Those are not the carrier. If you are keeping them, they leave the photograph. If a lead is leaving, it is not the box. Bag it or say “lead, separate”.

A plastic tray in the base may lift out. Lift it if it lifts. Empty it. Write “tray out, emptied” or “tray stuck”. Do not pour dirty litter or old food onto the store floor and then bag the mess with the carrier as if it were the box. Bag that waste on its own if it is ordinary household waste you are already discarding, and keep the bag out of the carrier. If you do not know what the residue is, leave the tray in, write “tray not emptied, contents unknown”, and do not hide it.

Do not hose the carrier in the store. Water in a windowless store has nowhere to go, and a wet box is heavier and still the same shape. This page does not include a wash. If the box smells, say so in plain words: “empty, smells, not washed”. Do not spray it with a chemical to hide that and then seal the door. A chemical in a closed plastic box is a different problem from an empty carrier.

Do not put documents, a passport, medication or someone else’s belongings inside to “use the space”. The carrier is leaving. Anything inside it leaves with it.

If the bedding is also leaving, it is its own line. Write “one bag of bedding, separate”. Do not stuff the bedding back in so the carrier looks like one soft item. A carrier full of towels is a box you have not emptied.

## The door that will not latch

The door is the part that makes this a carrier rather than a storage tub. It is also the part that sticks.

Look at the latch before you pull. A latch you can already open with a finger, the way you did when the carrier was in use, can be opened. Open it, and see whether it closes again. Write “door opens and latches” or “door opens, will not latch”.

If the latch will not move, do not hit it with a tool. Do not drill the plastic. Do not saw the grille out to make a hole. A stuck door stays stuck. Write “door will not open” and leave the grille on.

A grille that is already bent stays bent. Write “grille bent”. Do not bend it flat. A sharp wire end that is already exposed should be named: “wire end exposed, door left on”. Do not pull the wire out. Do not tape over it and then forget to say it is there. You may tape it only to cover the point you have already described, and the words stay in the note.

Some carriers split into a top and a base when clips on the sides are released. Release a clip only if it already moves by hand. If the two halves come apart, stack them only if they sit without sliding, and write “halves apart, stacked” or “halves apart, not stacked”. If a clip will not move, write “will not split” and leave it as one box. Do not lever a clip with a screwdriver until the plastic splits.

A top that lifts on a hinge may be opened to empty the box, then closed. If the hinge is cracked, do not force the top flat. Write “top hinge cracked, top left as it sits”.

Screws that hold a hinge, if they already turn by hand or with the screwdriver you use for that carrier, may be left as they are. This page does not ask you to rebuild the door. Do not remove the door to make the box lighter. A door in a separate bag is easy to lose, and the box is then a sharp-edged shell. Write “door still on”.

A carrier with no door, because the door was lost years ago, is still this note if the shell is the plastic carrier. Write “door missing”. Do not fit a random piece of wood across the opening.

## Where it sits in the store

The carrier is awkward because of where it was put, as well as because of the door.

On a high shelf, do not pull it down by the grille. Empty the shelf around it until you can lift the shell, or leave it on the shelf and write “on the high shelf, not brought down”. A carrier that falls splits, and a split box is a different shape from the one you measured. If you do bring it down, put it on the floor clear of the door.

Under a stack of boxes, move the boxes you are allowed to move. If a box is staying, do not bury the carrier again after you have photographed it. If you cannot reach the carrier without unpacking a wall of cartons you have not decided about, write “blocked by boxes” and photograph it where it is. Do not tip the wall.

Behind a rolled rug or a folded table, pull those aside if they move. The storage-room guide keeps the decision about the rug, the table and the rest of the room. This note is the carrier once you can see it. Do not call the corner “the carrier”.

Nested with a second carrier, separate them if they come apart by hand. Write “two carriers, separated” and give the second one its own line, with its own door and its own contents. If they will not come apart, write “two carriers, stuck nested” and do not kick them apart. Measure the pair as it is, and say there are two.

A carrier used as a shelf, with books or tools on the roof, is still a carrier. Take those things off. They are not the box. If a tool is leaving, it is its own line.

In a parking-bay locker, with its own key and its own route, the locker is the [parking-bay locker note](/blog/parking-bay-locker-clearance-dubai). This page stays the apartment store. If the only door you are clearing is that locker, use that note and do not describe the locker as a pet carrier.

Tied to a shelf upright with string, untie the string if it is loose. Write “string off”. If the carrier is screwed to a timber shelf that is part of the room, write “fixed to the shelf” and do not unscrew the building. This page starts when the carrier is already loose.

## One carrier, not the room

A second carrier is a second line. Do not tape three carriers into a tower and call it one item. Do not put a small carrier inside a large one unless it already fits without forcing, and then write “small one inside, both empty” only if that is true. If it does not fit, they stay separate.

The storage-room guide keeps the inventory, the path, and what stays. The arrival check keeps the general list for the day, the route, and what must not be left in a corridor. Stay on those pages when the question is the room or the visit. Stay on this page when the question is one plastic carrier. You can still use these steps on each carrier in a larger load, one at a time, so each one is empty and the door is described.

A bag of toys, a spare bowl and a folded bed, leaving as part of a wider clear-out, belong on the acceptance list as that wider load. This note stays the one carrier. Do not fill the carrier with the toys to save a line on the list.

Do not put a lead, a collar with a tag you still need, or kitchen rubbish inside and shut a door that will not open again. If the door will not open, the carrier has to be empty before you stop trying. If you already cannot open it, say what you believe is inside, and do not add more.

A carrier you are giving to someone who still wants it is not a collection. Arrange that handover yourself. Keep it out of the leaving photos until they have collected it. A message that says “the vet might take it” is not a handover. This business does not collect animals, and it does not place them.

The business removes items. It does not buy them, sell them or say what a carrier is worth.

A cot, a travel cot and a car seat are not this page. A cot is not given a URL here. Say the name of the object you actually have, and do not rename it to fit this note.

## Measure the carrier as it will travel

Measure after you have tried the door and the clips, not before, and not with a guess.

Length, width and height of the shell, including a grille that sticks out and a handle that stands up. If the handle already folds flat by hand, fold it and measure without it. If it will not fold, include it. If the two halves came apart, measure the stacked pair or each half, and say which you measured.

Then look at the opening it has to pass. The useful figures are the clear height and clear width of the store door, with the door as open as it goes. A carrier on a high shelf still has to pass that door if it is coming out. A box, a rug or a table leg can change the gap. Move what you are keeping, then measure if the gap changed.

If you have no tape, write “not measured”. Do not invent the numbers. This note does not keep a standard size for a carrier or for a store door.

Write one sentence you measured. “Door will not latch, shell wider than the store door” or “halves stacked, handle folded, not yet compared with the door” or “not measured”. Do not write “it will fit” if you have not compared the figures.

Weight is not something to guess. A carrier you cannot lift because it still has a wet pad in it is not a weight. Empty it first. If it is still too awkward for one person, write “awkward to lift”. Do not write a number you did not weigh.

The wider method for one object and the narrowest point on the way out stays with the [bulky-item note](/blog/bulky-item-pickup-dubai) if the corridor and the lift are the question. Stay on this page for the door, the contents and the store. Do not send two descriptions that disagree about whether the halves came apart.

Send these views.

- The latch or the clip that will not move, close enough to see it.
- The inside, empty, or the words “door will not open” and what you believe is still in it.

Add the room. Add the sentence about the door. Add a lift or loading note if you have been given one. If you have not, write “no lift instruction yet”. Do not invent a lift size, and do not write a building rule you have not been given.

## One line in the booking

You can [add it to the junk](/services/junk-removal) once it is empty or the stuck door is named, and the note says it is one plastic carrier. That page is a single pickup or a mixed household load. It is not a request to saw a grille, split a seized clip, or collect an animal.

Say, in one message:

- one plastic pet carrier, and where it sits in the store
- empty, or “door will not open” and what is still inside
- door latches, door will not latch, or door missing
- halves together, or halves apart
- the length, width and height you measured, or “not measured”
- anything else leaving from the same shelf, as its own line
- that it is not a stroller, not a playpen, and not a live animal

Do not write “pet stuff” as the only words if the store also holds a buggy, a bed and a bag of toys. Do not write “it is empty” if you have not looked under the pad. Do not ask for the shell to be cut because the figure is larger than the gap. The figure is the report. A cut is not a step on this page.

If you have not tried the latch, you are early. Try the movement the door already has. If it will not move, the stuck door is the honest line. Send that.

On the morning, put the carrier where the photos put it. Check the pad is still out. Check nobody has used it overnight as a box for something else. A towel pushed back inside is not an empty carrier. Take it out before you treat the old figure as current.

Point at the shell as the carrier. Point at a stroller, a playpen or a stack of boxes only if that object is its own line. The useful record is the sentence from the first check: one plastic pet carrier, emptied or the door named, kept as its own line, written in plain words.
